# Field Guide PWA (`apps/guide/`)

Guidance for the Field Guide PWA. Loaded when working with files under `apps/guide/`.
Repo-wide conventions, the editorial site, and the deployment model live in the root `CLAUDE.md`.

## PWA architecture

- **The look: instrument surfaces over journal prose (August 2026, the "Surveyor" pass).** The guide reads like a field instrument where it carries measured or live data and like a journal where it carries prose, and the split is the whole design. Prose keeps EB Garamond at its measure; **every label and every reading is set in JetBrains Mono** (`.eyebrow` and `.dateline` in `tokens.css` are the two classes that carry this app-wide, which is why a section heading anywhere in the app reads as an instrument label). No font was added: the Surveyor look comes from the mono readouts, the hairline panels and the granite ground, not from a display face, and adding one would cost every offline shell download ~30 KB for a face the brand does not otherwise use. The primitives live at the end of `styles/components.css`: `.panel` + `.readout` (a bordered block of live readings), `.spec-strip` + `.spec` (the measured facts about one stop), `.meter` (offline packs), and `.plate--hud` + `.plate__ticks` (photography framed as an instrument view, with corner brackets and a scrim). Two layout rules there are load-bearing and were both found by looking at the rendered page rather than the code: the panel's odd last cell takes `.readout--wide` and the spec strip is **flex, not grid**, because a trailing empty grid cell paints as an edge-coloured hole that reads exactly like a reading that failed to load; and `.spec` wraps two-to-a-row (`flex: 1 1 150px`) with `white-space: nowrap` on the label, because four to a row fits the labels but breaks `4,400 ft` and the coordinate mid-token, and a reading split across two lines is no longer scannable.
- **Colour scheme is the reader's, not the device's (`lib/theme.ts`, the Account card).** Three values in `tfg.theme` — `auto` (default, follows the device), `daylight` (cream paper), `granite` (dark) — applied as `data-theme` on `<html>`, which `tokens.css` reads. `main.tsx` calls `applyTheme(readTheme())` **before render**, or the app paints the device's scheme and swaps under the reader. Auto resolves to daylight unless the device is in dark mode, and that asymmetry is deliberate: a cream ground outreads a dark one in direct sun and this guide is used outdoors, so granite is opt-in in daylight. The granite palette is declared **twice** in `tokens.css` (once under `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` for Auto, once under `:root[data-theme='granite']` for the pin) because CSS cannot share one declaration list across a media query and a plain selector; keep them in sync. An explicit choice also has to rewrite **both** `<meta name="theme-color">` tags, whose `media` attributes answer the device rather than the reader.
- **`components/ParkNowPanel.tsx` is the front page's hero**: the four readings a visitor checks before deciding anything (valley forecast, today's light, roads, entrance waits), composed from feeds the app already has plus the on-device sun calculation. It fetches nothing of its own — all four hooks share one in-flight request per feed at module level. **Every cell is independently honest**: a feed that is missing, unreachable, or stale past its own HIDE window drops its cell rather than showing a dash or a zero, because a wrong reading here sends somebody up a closed road. The sun cell is computed on the device and can never be stale, which is what guarantees the panel is never empty and never a moving layout anchor; it turns over to tomorrow's sunrise once the sun is down. Roads and waits moved off Home's line stack into this panel (air and river flow stayed as lines, being footnotes rather than things a trip pivots on), so the panel is the only place they render on `/` — do not re-add `RoadsLine`/`WaitsLine` there without removing the panel's cells. Directly under it sits the trip strip, and the masthead carries an offline chip (`OfflineChip` in GatedChrome: pack count until complete, then "Offline ready"); the Home page's region rows deliberately dropped their italic teasers — the seasonal facts they carried live on the region pages and, live, in the panel.
- **Content model.** `apps/guide/src/content/schema.ts` defines `Stop` (zod schema) with a `region` enum: `valley` | `glacier-mariposa` | `tuolumne` | `hetch-hetchy`. `content/stops.ts` is the seed array, validated at module load via `Stops.parse(seed)` — schema violations throw in the Vite overlay or fail the build. `content/index.ts` exports `REGIONS` (the picker metadata), `REGION_SHORT` (chip-length labels), and helpers `getStopsByRegion`, `getStopById`, `getRegionMeta`. `content/itineraries.ts` defines 1/2/3-day presets whose day stop lists are derived live from `getStopsByRegion()` — adding a stop to a region flows through with no itinerary edit (the presets cover the original three regions; Hetch Hetchy is a la carte). `content/seasonal.ts` is the bundled seasonal almanac (full moons, road-opening windows, firefall): entries convert to ProgramEvent records (source `seasonal`, an enum value mirrored into the Worker schema) and merge into /programs client-side, so the agenda keeps an offline floor; `typical`-confidence entries are labeled as such everywhere they render.
- **Prepackaged plans and the clock (August 2026).** `content/itineraries.ts` presets carry one ordered `plan` per day — stop ids and day-hike ids interleaved in drive order, resolved by `resolvePlanEntry` (core stop, then hike, then hidden stop: `mcgurk-meadow` is deliberately both a hidden stop and a hike, and a preset naming it means the hike). The two arrays it replaced could only seed hikes after every stop, which put McGurk Meadow an hour back up the road it had just come down. **A day without a `plan` derives from `getStopsByRegion()`, which is a reading order, not a day** — the two days that relied on it lost Wapama Falls and Soda Springs to the capacity filter and put lunch at 6:54 p.m., so treat the fallback as a safety net, not an option. Sequence comes from the plan, but not every time: a stop whose `dayPart` (content/schema.ts) says the clock is a fact gets anchored by `trip/slotting.ts`. Three values, and the difference between the last two is load-bearing — `midday` (a meal) and `sunset` (a stop whose value is the last light, timed off `sun/solar.ts` so December anchors to 4:42 p.m. and June to 8:23) are **reserved before the day is filled**, so two seeded ranger programs move the day around them instead of walking lunch to 5 p.m.; `evening` (the meal on the way out) is only a **floor** placed last, because reserving a December dinner would sit it in front of the five-hour Wapama hike and push the hike off the day. `dayPart` is opt-in and narrow: a stop that is merely *better* early (Mirror Lake's reflection) carries none — that is the drive order's job. `apps/guide/scripts/check-itineraries.ts` (`npm --prefix scripts run check`, or `run itineraries:check`) seeds and slots every preset across five dates through the app's own modules, and fails on a lunch outside midday, a sunset stop that misses sunset, an entry the capacity filter drops, or an anchored stop pushed off the day. Midwinter is exempt from the everything-must-fit rule only (three and a half fewer hours of light); anchored stops are never exempt.
- **Archive notes on stops (July 2026).** A Stop may carry an optional `history`: one short, sourced note from the Nature Notes archive (see the Nature Notes archive bullet in the root `CLAUDE.md`) with the citing issue's `volume`, `number`, and printed `issueDate`. `content/archive.ts` builds the link from those three values (`/archive/<year>/vol-<v>-no-<n>/` on the **editorial** origin, year taken off the end of `issueDate`), and `components/ArchiveNote.tsx` renders it under the body on the full read only (`StopCard` with `compact={false}`: the stop page and `/preview`) — a citation block on every card in a region list turns the list into a bibliography. Two rules, both load-bearing: **every fact in the note must come from the cited issue** (the citation is the whole point, and a reader who follows the link has to find what the note claims), and the link is an external one that will not open offline, which is why the note itself is bundled and only the source needs the network. `scripts/check-archive-citations.mjs` (wired into `npm --prefix scripts run check`) scans the seed files and fails if a citation points at an archive page that does not exist or that does not print the date the note claims — nothing at runtime can catch either.
- **Coords are web-verified, not ground-truthed.** A July 2026 pass checked every `coord` in `stops.ts` against NPS place/trailhead pages, USGS GNIS, and OSM-derived sources; each coord line carries its source and any prior offset. Stops still marked `// TODO: verify on the ground` (unsigned pullouts, off-trail features, conflicting sources) have no authoritative source; never strip those markers without a real field visit, because the field guide's value prop depends on accurate parking turnouts.
- **Routes** (see `App.tsx`): `/open` (magic-link landing), `/login`, `/` (the front-page index of the whole guide, in Surveyor order: the ParkNowPanel conditions readout, the always-present trip strip (`.trip-strip`, the planner's front door alongside the tab bar), the region index rows with the Secret Guide as their fifth entry, the instrument tiles (map/hikes/programs/night), the eating and reference directories, the offline-packs meter panel, then the numbered "how this guide works" strip and the seasonal almanac; saved stops last), `/welcome` (first-run orientation: offline explainer, install step, download packs; `/open` and `/` route here until `tfg.onboarded` is set), `/region/:regionId`, `/stop/:stopId`, `/essentials` + `/essentials/:topicId` (know-before-you-go, incl. the `before-you-go` night-before checklist and the packing checklist), `/secret-guide` (the merged premium section: region-less secret spots + hidden-collection stops, filtered by `SecretCategory` tabs; `/secret-spots` and `/hidden-areas` redirect here), `/search`, `/programs` (trip-dates → date-grouped program list), `/hikes` (the bundled day-hike catalog in `content/hikes.ts` — every in-park day hike with distance/gain/difficulty, stats compiled from NPS pages + the yosemitehikes.com index with original descriptions; a third `TripItem` type `hike` flows through the planner, slotting, and ICS like a stop), `/hike/:hikeId` (granular trail page: verified stats, interactive elevation profile, derived effort score, GPX export, track-on-map link — see "trail tracks" below), `/trip` (the trip board: see "trip board" below; the calendar export below it saves a one-time ICS file and nothing else, see "calendar export" below; `?import=<ids>` accepts a trip handed over from the editorial map, see "trip hand-off" below), `/trip/print` (the printable one-page plan), `/dining` (every in-park venue plus the gateway-town corridors, from `content/dining.ts`), `/wildlife` (the quick-ID guide, `content/wildlife.ts`: field marks, no photos by design), `/hunts` (the junior naturalist find-it lists, `content/hunts.ts`, check-off on the shared `tfg.checklist` map), `/night` (the night-sky module: on-device lunar/solar math plus the almanac; only the Tioga road note reads a live feed), `/this-week` (the "we're here this week" composition of the almanac plus the live alerts/air/flow lines — composition only, it fetches nothing the rest of the app does not), `/today` (the read-only field-day view for the current trip day: conditions (day forecast + computed sun times + live entrance waits), the live park webcams (`components/WebcamStrip.tsx`: the guide's only third-party image load, absent offline and under Data Saver, and never cached by the SW because its fetch handler returns early for cross-origin requests), a Now/Next-up card with a directions deeplink, and the day's slotted blocks in time order; entry points are a Home card and a `/trip` link shown only while the trip window includes today), `/map` (MapLibre GL over the Worker's `/tiles` raster topo proxy — NOT Google Maps; pin styles and the Google Maps directions deeplink live in `src/map/kinds.ts`), `/account` (DownloadManager), `/redeem` (public newsletter promo-code redemption: posts the Worker's `/api/redeem`, prefills the code from `?code=`, and ends at "check your email" — access always arrives by email, never as a direct sign-in, because the inbox is the proof of ownership). Everything except `/open`, `/login`, `/preview`, and `/redeem` is wrapped in `RequireAuth`; `/stop/:stopId` is gated by `StopGate` instead, which renders the normal stop page signed in and a public marketing teaser signed out. `/trip/*` subpaths redirect to `/trip`.
- **Public storefront surfaces (July 2026).** `/preview` is the free sample: five real entries (one stop per region + one secret spot, ids in `lib/storefront.ts`) rendered in full by the same `StopCard` (with `actions={false}`), locked-count dividers, and buy CTAs. Signed-out `/stop/:id` visits render `StopTeaser` (title/photo/teaser only; sample ids read in full) so links buyers share become landing pages — `ShareStopButton` on stop pages feeds this loop. `/login` carries a sell block, and the access-ended card offers "buy again" (the Stripe webhook overwrites the buyer record, so a repeat purchase genuinely renews). All buy actions link OUT to the editorial `/guide` page (`GUIDE_BUY_URL`); the price renders live via `useGuidePrice()` from the unauthenticated `/api/inventory`, `$3.99` fallback. The editorial buy box links back with `guide_sample_click`. Keep gated routes gated: the sample is `StopGate`/`isPreviewStopId` only.
- **Card deck (July 2026).** `/region/:regionId` and `/secret-guide` default to a swipe deck instead of a long scroll: one entry per screen, swipe up for the next, TikTok-style. `components/CardDeck.tsx` is the mechanism (a `scroll-snap-type: y mandatory` scroller, **native scroll, not a hand-rolled pointer drag** — the browser owns the fling and nothing can steal the gesture mid-drag; panels are exactly viewport-height, which is what makes `scrollTop / clientHeight` a trustworthy index), `components/StopDeckCard.tsx` is the one-screen card (photo, title, meta chips, clamped teaser, the same trip/save/visited actions as `StopCard` via the shared `StopActions`; the full read stays at `/stop/:id`). Both pages keep the original list, reachable from the Cards/List toggle (`components/ViewToggle.tsx`, `lib/viewMode.ts`, one global preference), and every block that lives outside the cards in list mode (region header + forecast, the region's Secret Guide links, back links) becomes an opening or closing panel in the deck, so no content is view-mode-only. The Secret Guide's `#<id>` deep links open the deck on that entry (`startKey`).
- **Onboarding** (`lib/onboarding.ts`, `routes/Welcome.tsx`): flag `tfg.onboarded`, deliberately **fails open** — a storage read error counts as onboarded so storage-denied browsers aren't trapped on `/welcome`. Only the front page redirects there; deep links are never intercepted.
- **PWA map** (`routes/Map.tsx`): one MapLibre instance created once and never unmounted; URL carries `?tab=&itinerary=&stop=&kinds=&secret=`. Pin-kind filter chips with live counts (`null` set = "All"; validate `?kinds=` with `Object.hasOwn`, not `in`), a Secret Guide toggle, a "Near you" list of the five closest *visible* stops (`utils/geo.ts` haversine; the one thing left in the points pane since browse-by-area was removed, so the pane renders only when there is a fix or a location-denied note and docks as a bottom handle on phones), and map-only amenity pins (parking/campgrounds from `AMENITIES`, separate marker pipeline, no trip actions).
- **Trail tracks (July 2026).** Per-hike GPS tracks + elevation profiles in `apps/guide/public/tracks/<hikeId>.json`, generated by `scripts/gen-hike-tracks.mjs` (`npm --prefix scripts run tracks`; needs network, caches ~100 MB of USGS quads plus an Overture extract in the gitignored `scripts/data/.trailcache/`). Geometry is USGS National Map trail linework (NPS source data) routed via per-hike waypoint specs in `scripts/data/hike-track-specs.mjs`; the 13 hikes whose USGS linework is missing or maps a retired alignment carry `source: 'osm'` and route over OpenStreetMap linework instead (via the Overture Maps GeoParquet on S3, `scripts/lib/overture-trails.mjs`; © OSM contributors, ODbL — each track file states its source). Elevations are USGS 3DEP terrain tiles. **Every track is validated against the published distance/gain in `hikes.ts` (a spec's `gainDef` picks which definition the published gain uses: net, one-way, or round-trip cumulative) — all 57 hikes now ship tracks, and a route that failed validation would ship without one, never with an approximate line (statuses in `scripts/data/trail-tracks-report.md`).** The generated `src/content/trails.generated.ts` (stats index + `TRACKS_VERSION` content hash) is committed alongside the JSONs; the `?v=` hash on track URLs is what turns over the SW's cache-first runtime cache, so regenerate (not hand-edit) after any hikes.ts stat change. App side: `src/trails/` (schema mirror, `useTrack` loader, GPX export), `/hike/:hikeId` detail page, `?hike=` track overlay on `/map`, and a "Trail tracks & elevation" download pack. Track JSONs are runtime-cache assets in `sw.js` (`/tracks/` pattern) and honor the never-cache-HTML invariant.
- **Weather feature** (`src/weather/`). Same shape as programs: `schema.ts` mirrors `workers/src/lib/weather.ts` by hand, `useWeather` is network-first against `/api/weather` with a Cache API fallback (payload in `tfg-data` under a synthetic key, stamp in `tfg.weather.meta`). Renders as a one-line current forecast on each Home region row and as `WeatherStrip` on region pages (the five-day `RegionForecast` block plus the next four NWS periods) — not on stop pages. Weather is garnish, never an error: stale > 12 h gets a "forecast is old" label, stale > 48 h renders nothing (`staleness.ts`). `forecastDays.ts` folds NWS day/night half-periods into park-local calendar days.
- **Live condition lines (alerts / air / flow).** `src/alerts/`, `src/air/`, and `src/flow/` each mirror the weather feature's shape exactly (hand-mirrored zod schema of the Worker route, network-first hook with a Cache API fallback, staleness gating, one-line render component that renders nothing rather than an error). All four hooks share one in-flight request per feed at module level, because two surfaces on the same page (e.g. `/this-week` plus its `RoadsLine`) used to issue the same GET twice.
- **Entrance waits + sun times (July 2026).** `src/waits/` mirrors the weather feature's shape (hand-mirrored zod schema of `/api/waits`, network-first `useWaits`) but deliberately keeps **no offline cache and no meta stamp**: waits swing on a 15-minute scale, so past `HIDE_AFTER_MS` (60 min) the line renders nothing, and every failure (including 404 while the Worker route is not yet deployed) resolves silently to empty. `WaitsLine` renders on Home under the region rows and on `/today`. `src/sun/solar.ts` is a dependency-free NOAA solar calculation (one fixed Valley coordinate, park-local minutes-from-midnight, ±2 min vs the NOAA calculator incl. DST edges); `SunLine` renders on `/today` and as the always-present floor of `WeatherStrip` ("Weather & light"), the one deliberate loosening of the weather-renders-nothing rule, allowed only because computed sun times cannot be stale.
- **Auth state.** JWT lives in `localStorage` under key `tfg.jwt` (`auth/storage.ts`). Session shape: `{ jwt, username }` — the JWT `sub` claim is treated as a string label and surfaced as `username` regardless of which Worker auth path issued it. The session is derived from the JWT itself (`sessionFromJwt`), not by re-reading localStorage, so sign-in works in storage-blocked browsers without burning single-use magic-link tokens.
- **PWA localStorage keys** beyond `tfg.jwt`: `tfg.accessEndedAt`, `tfg.me`, `tfg.theme` (colour scheme: `auto` | `daylight` | `granite`), `tfg.downloads` (pack completion), `tfg.favorites`, `tfg.visited` (visited-stop ids), `tfg.stopNotes` (private per-stop notes), `tfg.checklist`, `tfg.install.dismissed`, `tfg.install.shown` (the one-time auto-open of the install sheet), `tfg.beforeYouGo.dismissed`, `tfg.onboarded`, `tfg.viewMode` (cards vs list on the deck surfaces), `tfg.deck.hint` (the swipe hint, shown once), `tfg.trip.dates` (shared by `/programs` and `/trip`), `tfg.trip.plan` (the planner, zod-parsed drop-on-corrupt), `tfg.trip.density` (trip board zoom), `tfg.sync.enabled` / `tfg.sync.localAt` / `tfg.sync.lastAt` (cross-device sync: the opt-in, the change clock for the stores that carry no stamp of their own, and the display stamp), `tfg.push.enabled` (notification opt-in), `tfg.trip.pendingImport` (a trip handed over from the editorial map before the buyer owned the guide), `tfg.programs.meta` and `tfg.weather.meta` (cache stamps). Cache API buckets: `tfg-data` (programs + weather payloads, unversioned, survives deploys), `tfg-tiles` (map tiles), `tfg-runtime` (photos/fonts/icons), `tfg-shell-<version>` (app shell, rotated per deploy).
- **API calls** go through `apiFetch` (`lib/api.ts`), which applies a 15-second `AbortSignal.timeout` by default (captive-portal Wi-Fi accepts connections and never answers); a timeout rethrows as a plain network `Error`, not `ApiError`. Callers passing their own `signal` keep control.
- **Cross-device sync salvages, and salvage never writes a blank.** `sync/schema.ts` re-validates every pulled document because one written by a newer build is exactly the case that must not corrupt this one, and it repairs rather than rejects: a document whose plan went bad still carries usable favorites, visited marks, and notes. The rule that makes that safe is that a field the parse could **not** read is flagged in `unparseable` and then **skipped** by `applyRemote`, never written. The repaired document carries an empty placeholder so the envelope parses, and applying a placeholder over real data is a wipe: it took out every saved stop, visited mark, and note on the device, and then the heal push carried the loss to every other device (CODE-AUDIT-2026-08 §2). Three parts hold it up. (1) A list survives only **intact** — filtering out the entries that fail and keeping the rest is the same wipe in slow motion, since a newer build storing `[{id, addedAt}]` filters down to zero strings. (2) An **absent** field counts as unparseable, not as "the other device has none": every build that has written this document writes all four. The plan is the one exception, because `null` is a valid plan. (3) The heal push (`canHeal` in `planSync.ts`) fires only when **nothing** was lost, for the mirror reason: pushing our version of a field we could not read destroys the newer build's copy and re-clobbers it every pass. It used to guard the plan alone, which is how the other three were left exposed.
- **Programs feature.** `src/programs/schema.ts` mirrors `workers/src/lib/programs.ts` (keep in sync by hand — no shared package on purpose). `usePrograms` is network-first against `/api/programs` with an offline fallback to the cached window. Two of the event fields are the **printed Yosemite Guide's own per-program symbols**, carried through the feed and rendered as the "Wheelchair accessible" and "All ages" badges (plus the accessible filter chip, which only appears when the window holds marked events): `accessible` and `familyFriendly` are **true-only**, because the Guide declining to mark a program is not a claim that it is inaccessible or wrong for kids — never render a "not accessible" state from their absence, and never infer either flag from a program's category. The NPS Events API carries neither, so they only ever arrive on hand-curated entries. The trip planner (`src/trip/`) snapshots program events into the plan so it survives cache eviction; `trip/ics.ts` hand-rolls the ICS (Pacific VTIMEZONE, folded lines, stable UIDs, VALARM reminders on timed events, calendar-level X-WR-CALNAME) and `trip/exportTrip.ts` prefers Web Share with a File (iOS standalone PWA can't reliably download blobs) before falling back to an anchor download.
- **Trip board (`/trip`).** The plan renders as a calendar board (`components/TripAgenda.tsx` + `.css`), not a list: one full-width timeline per day of the trip window (empty days included, so they can be dropped onto), blocks positioned and sized from `trip/slotting.ts` output at a persisted px-per-minute zoom (`tfg.trip.density`), tinted per item by `trip/agendaItem.ts` (stop tones come from the map's `KIND_STYLES`, so a viewpoint is the same hue on the board and on `/map`; programs key off their category), and lane-packed by `trip/agendaLayout.ts` when two things overlap. Every block carries its coordinate and a directions deeplink. **Dragging is hand-rolled on pointer events, and the touch path is load-bearing:** a block's body is `touch-action: none`, because a block that lets the browser scroll has the gesture taken away mid-drag (Chrome fires `pointercancel` on the first move after the lift, which is exactly the failure this replaced), so the board scrolls those touches itself, fling included — do not "fix" that back to `pan-y`. Press-and-hold lifts on touch, 4 px of travel lifts with a mouse; dropping calls `placeItem` (one write for day + time) and pins the item so slotting stops moving it, and the bottom-edge handle calls `setItemDuration`. Arrow keys, Alt+arrows, and PageUp/PageDown are the keyboard equivalents. Day windows carry an hour of headroom past the first and last block (`dayWindowFor`), without which a drag has nowhere later to go and clamps straight back.
- **Calendar export.** Exactly one path, and deliberately so: the trip review's footer (`components/TripReview.tsx`) renders the plan to an `.ics` client-side and hands it to the OS through `trip/exportTrip.ts`. It is a one-time file, fully offline, and the copy says plainly that a later plan edit means saving the file again. The Google Calendar OAuth push (Worker `/api/calendar/google/*`, `trip/googleCalendar.ts`, `trip/calendarSync.ts`, the Account page's `CalendarCard`) and the hosted subscription feed (`/api/trip/feed`, `trip/feed.ts`, `trip/feedSync.ts`, the webcal/add-by-URL sheet) were both **removed** in July 2026 — do not reintroduce a server-side calendar integration without a decision to reverse that. Nothing about a buyer's calendar touches the Worker now, so there is no OAuth client to keep verified and no capability URL to revoke. Slotting travel buffers are distance-derived (`trip/slotting.ts`: haversine at a 22 mph park heuristic + park-and-walk, clamp 10-75 min, flat 30 when a coord is missing).
- **Service worker** (`public/sw.js`) caches the app shell + photos for offline use. **Version-coherence invariant (August 2026):** the navigation handler re-caches fetched HTML under the running worker's `SHELL_CACHE`, but only after `shellAssetsCached()` confirms every `/assets/*` URL that HTML references is already in some cache — right after a deploy the OLD worker fetches the NEW build's HTML, and storing that against the old chunk set was a white screen on the next offline launch. The asset handler's counterpart: on a miss in its own shell cache it tries `caches.match()` across all caches before the network (hashed URLs are content-addressed, so a cross-version hit is always correct). Install retries each fetch once and deletes its partial cache on failure, so one dropped connection on park wifi no longer scraps a whole update. The `UpdateBanner` component prompts when a new build is available; the "an update is waiting" fact is **latched in `pwa/registerSW.ts` module state** (`getPendingUpdate`/`onUpdateReady`), not delivered as a one-shot event, because an update found during boot fires before the banner subscribes and a remount used to drop it — both read as "the update bar disappeared". `registerSW` also reads `registration.installing` directly (the spec fires `updatefound` *before* `register()` resolves, so a listener attached in the `.then` misses the first one), keys "is this an update" off `registration.active` rather than `navigator.serviceWorker.controller` (a hard reload loads the page uncontrolled), and re-announces a still-waiting worker after every poll/focus `update()` — which is what brings the bar back if it was ever lost. **Hard invariant since the July 2026 audit:** an HTML response (the SPA `_redirects` 200 fallback) must never be cached under an asset/photo/tile/shell key — the SW and `useDownloads.fetchIntoCache` both guard with an `isHtml` check and treat such a response as a failed URL, and `activate` self-heals by purging HTML from the runtime and tile caches. Photo pre-warming goes through `pwa/precache.ts` (`PRECACHE_URLS` message, prod-only, works pre-controller via `serviceWorker.ready` with a 10 s timeout). The `beforeinstallprompt` event is captured at module level in `pwa/installPrompt.ts`, called from `main.tsx` **before render** (component listeners fire too late); `Welcome.tsx` and `InstallPrompt.tsx` consume it via `useDeferredInstallPrompt`. **iOS has no such event and never has**, so nothing can make Safari show an OS install dialog: the ask there is the app's own `components/InstallSheet.tsx`, which gives per-browser Add to Home Screen steps (`iosBrowser()` in `utils/platform.ts` tells Safari from Chrome/Firefox/Edge from an embedded webview, where installing is impossible and the only useful instruction is "open this in Safari"). `lib/install.ts` owns the two flags and `shouldAutoPrompt()`, whose claim is memoized in module state because a `useState` initializer runs twice under StrictMode and the banner remounts on every route change — either would burn the one unprompted open. Entry points: the sheet auto-opens once after onboarding, the banner's "Show me" reopens it, `/welcome` step 2 opens it (and claims the auto-open so Home doesn't ask again a tap later), and the Account page's `InstallCard` un-dismisses it for a buyer who tapped "Not now" and changed their mind. `vite.config.ts` pins React and the router into a `react-vendor` manual chunk so it survives shell re-precache across deploys.
- **Nothing in the build may read the clock (August 2026).** The Pages project rebuilds on every push to `main`, and most of those pushes change nothing in this app — the nightly `chore: record nightly Lighthouse run [skip ci]` commit is the usual one, and `[skip ci]` is a GitHub Actions convention that Cloudflare Pages does not honor. So any wall-clock value that reaches `sw.js` or a hashed chunk turns every no-op redeploy into a real update for every installed copy: a rotated shell cache, a full re-download over park LTE, and an "Updated. Tap to refresh." bar for a build with no changes in it. Two did, and both are fixed: the SW cache name was a full ISO timestamp (so `sw.js` differed on *every* build, verified as two builds fifteen seconds apart), and `VITE_BUILD_DATE` was `define`d into the bundle, where it landed in the main chunk and the Account chunk and moved their content hashes at every midnight. The SW version is now a **content hash** of the SW template, the API base, and the sorted asset filenames — which is exactly the question the browser asks when it byte-compares `sw.js`, so a real change (app code, CSS, the SW itself) still fires the banner, same-day hotfixes included, and an unchanged rebuild correctly announces nothing. The asset list is sorted because `readdir` order is filesystem-dependent and that list is both baked into the SW and hashed into its version, and `dist/index.html` is deliberately *not* hashed in, because it carries the build stamp and its freshness never depended on the shell version anyway (the navigation handler is network-first and re-caches it on every online load). The stamp itself moved out of the bundle into a `tfg-build-date` meta tag on `index.html`, read once by `lib/buildInfo.ts`, and now comes from the last commit that actually touched `apps/guide` rather than the day CI ran — which is also the honest answer to the "Last updated" line a buyer reads on Home. `scripts/check-build-determinism.mjs` (`npm run check:build`, wired into `.github/workflows/ci.yml` after the build) builds twice and demands byte-identical output; it is the only thing that can catch a regression here, because by the time a clock read reaches a phone it is a genuinely different file and nothing at runtime can tell it from a real update.
- **Download packs** (`offline/useDownloads.ts`): live status and abort controllers are module state so in-flight downloads survive remounts; `verifyPack` tolerates a small missing fraction (5% for the tile pack) and records tolerated failures rather than demanding 100%. A failed verification also **clears the pack's `tfg.downloads` completion entry** — `isPackCompleted()` feeds Home's airplane-mode line and the map's Downloaded badge, and a flag the cache no longer backs (iOS eviction) would keep both claiming offline capability the device lost. Quota exhaustion is named as "device is out of storage", not "check your connection", and the Download-everything batch stops on the first error rather than printing the same failure seven times.
- **Stop photos are a placeholder pass.** `public/photos/` reuses editorial-site images (several stops share the same photo); many stops still have no matching image and need dedicated photography before launch. An entry with none carries `photos: []` and renders the "Photo coming" tile. The one hard rule: **never wire a `src` at a file that is not in `public/photos/` yet.** ResponsivePhoto degrades to that same tile, so a dangling reference looks merely unfinished, but photo download packs carry `tolerateMissing: 0` (every photo is paid content), so one missing file makes its region's offline download fail permanently and blames the buyer's connection — which is exactly what three dangling srcs did to the Yosemite Valley pack until July 2026. `scripts/check-guide-photos.mjs` (in `npm --prefix scripts run check`; `run photos:check` for the full inventory) fails on a dangling src or a gap in a photo's responsive ladder, and warns on a photo with no `PHOTO_CREDITS` entry.

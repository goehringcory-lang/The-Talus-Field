# Field Guide PWA (`apps/guide/`)

Guidance for the Field Guide PWA. Loaded when working with files under `apps/guide/`.
Repo-wide conventions, the editorial site, and the deployment model live in the root `CLAUDE.md`.

## PWA architecture

- **The look: instrument surfaces over journal prose (August 2026, the "Surveyor" pass).** The guide reads like a field instrument where it carries measured or live data and like a journal where it carries prose, and the split is the whole design. Prose keeps EB Garamond at its measure; **every label and every reading is set in JetBrains Mono** (`.eyebrow` and `.dateline` in `tokens.css` are the two classes that carry this app-wide, which is why a section heading anywhere in the app reads as an instrument label). No font was added: the Surveyor look comes from the mono readouts, the hairline panels and the granite ground, not from a display face, and adding one would cost every offline shell download ~30 KB for a face the brand does not otherwise use. The primitives live at the end of `styles/components.css`: `.panel` + `.readout` (a bordered block of live readings), `.spec-strip` + `.spec` (the measured facts about one stop), `.meter` (offline packs), and `.plate--hud` + `.plate__ticks` (photography framed as an instrument view, with corner brackets and a scrim). Two layout rules there are load-bearing and were both found by looking at the rendered page rather than the code: the panel's odd last cell takes `.readout--wide` and the spec strip is **flex, not grid**, because a trailing empty grid cell paints as an edge-coloured hole that reads exactly like a reading that failed to load; and `.spec` wraps two-to-a-row (`flex: 1 1 150px`) with `white-space: nowrap` on the label, because four to a row fits the labels but breaks `4,400 ft` and the coordinate mid-token, and a reading split across two lines is no longer scannable.
- **The palette is the site's design palette (September 2026 home redesign).** Cream paper `#f8f5ed`, deep green ink `#233e35`, one rust accent `#a4462c` (still `--moss`/`--rust`, so no screen restates a colour), a `#fffdf7` card fill (`--panel`) and `#d6dacf` hairlines (`--rule-soft`); granite is a green-black `#141d19` with the accent lifted to `#e2825c`. The ground is flat (`--field-texture: none`). `--band*` tokens carry the one dark band on a light page (the Park Bulletin link). The literals that mirror the tokens by hand moved with them: `index.html` and `manifest.webmanifest` (theme and background colour), `lib/theme.ts` (`CHROME`), `map/theme.ts` (fallbacks and road casing) and the offline page in `public/sw.js`. The field card (`lib/fieldCard.ts`) keeps its own frozen granite values on purpose, and the Secret Guide's photo scrim and the cream type laid on it (`--sg-scrim`, `--sg-on-photo*` in `components/SecretGuide.css`) are literals because they sit on a photograph, which looks the same in either scheme. A colour change means all of those, not just `tokens.css`.
- **Colour scheme is the reader's, not the device's (`lib/theme.ts`, the Account card).** Three values in `tfg.theme` — `auto` (default, follows the device), `daylight` (cream paper), `granite` (dark) — applied as `data-theme` on `<html>`, which `tokens.css` reads. `main.tsx` calls `applyTheme(readTheme())` **before render**, or the app paints the device's scheme and swaps under the reader. Auto resolves to daylight unless the device is in dark mode, and that asymmetry is deliberate: a cream ground outreads a dark one in direct sun and this guide is used outdoors, so granite is opt-in in daylight. The granite palette is declared **twice** in `tokens.css` (once under `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` for Auto, once under `:root[data-theme='granite']` for the pin) because CSS cannot share one declaration list across a media query and a plain selector; keep them in sync. An explicit choice also has to rewrite **both** `<meta name="theme-color">` tags, whose `media` attributes answer the device rather than the reader.
- **`components/ParkNowPanel.tsx` is the front page's opening unit** ("Yosemite, right now", redrawn again in the September 2026 home redesign, `.glance*` in `components/ParkNowPanel.css`). A heading (the park's date and hour, an `h1`), one card of three blocks in the order the redesign fixed (**weather, light, entrance waits**), and the dark Park Bulletin band linking out to the editorial `/now` with the known road statuses on its face. Weather is the valley's current NWS period (temperature, condition glyph from `weather/conditionKind.ts`, high and low, wind, rain chance) over a strip of the next four periods (`weather/periodName.ts` shortens "Monday Night" to "Mon night"). Light is a **horizon scene**: a flat horizon, an outlined half-sun at sunrise and a filled one at sunset, and between them the sun where the clock puts it (`sun/sunScene.ts`: x along the day, y a sine of the elapsed fraction), with the light so far drawn as a solid line along the horizon; before sunrise and after sunset only the half-suns and the line remain, and the sunrise column turns over to tomorrow's. The sunset column's second line reads "Alpenglow begins at" the computed golden-hour start (an owner call; it is the time golden hour starts, not a separate alpenglow calculation). Waits are one column per entrance the feed lists (name, highway from `waits/waitWord.ts`, minutes, a bar scaled to an hour, and the word Short, Moderate or Long; Long is 30 minutes and up and is the alert colour). The Lots cell stays gone: most lots read `unknown` most days, and lot status renders on map pins and the stops and hikes that park there. It fetches nothing of its own: all four hooks share one in-flight request per feed at module level. **Every block is independently honest**: a feed that is missing, unreachable, or stale past its own HIDE window drops its block rather than showing a dash or a zero, because a wrong reading here sends somebody up a closed road; the light block is computed on the device and can never be stale, which is what keeps the card from being empty or a moving layout anchor. Every live block names its source and age. Roads and waits render only here on `/`; do not re-add `RoadsLine`/`WaitsLine` there without removing the panel's blocks. Directly under the band sit the one-time notes (`home-notes`), then the trip card (`components/TripDaysCard.tsx`, see "the region planner" below); the masthead carries the offline chip. The region cards are one-line photo cards that open the region page (`RegionCards.tsx`), and the Secret Guide closes them as a plate (photo, a live entry count, the five sections as numbered rows into `/secret-guide?cat=`, one button).
- **Content model.** `apps/guide/src/content/schema.ts` defines `Stop` (zod schema) with a `region` enum: `valley` | `glacier-mariposa` | `tuolumne` | `hetch-hetchy`. `content/stops.ts` is the seed array, validated at module load via `Stops.parse(seed)` — schema violations throw in the Vite overlay or fail the build. `content/index.ts` exports `REGIONS` (the picker metadata), `REGION_SHORT` (chip-length labels), and helpers `getStopsByRegion`, `getStopById`, `getRegionMeta`. `content/itineraries.ts` defines 1/2/3-day presets whose day stop lists are derived live from `getStopsByRegion()` — adding a stop to a region flows through with no itinerary edit (the presets cover the original three regions; Hetch Hetchy is a la carte). `content/seasonal.ts` is the bundled seasonal almanac (full moons, road-opening windows, firefall): entries convert to ProgramEvent records (source `seasonal`, an enum value mirrored into the Worker schema) and merge into /programs client-side, so the agenda keeps an offline floor; `typical`-confidence entries are labeled as such everywhere they render.
- **Prepackaged plans and the clock (August 2026).** `content/itineraries.ts` presets carry one ordered `plan` per day — stop ids and day-hike ids interleaved in drive order, resolved by `resolvePlanEntry` (core stop, then hike, then hidden stop: `mcgurk-meadow` is deliberately both a hidden stop and a hike, and a preset naming it means the hike). The two arrays it replaced could only seed hikes after every stop, which put McGurk Meadow an hour back up the road it had just come down. **A day without a `plan` derives from `getStopsByRegion()`, which is a reading order, not a day** — the two days that relied on it lost Wapama Falls and Soda Springs to the capacity filter and put lunch at 6:54 p.m., so treat the fallback as a safety net, not an option. Sequence comes from the plan, but not every time: a stop whose `dayPart` (content/schema.ts) says the clock is a fact gets anchored by `trip/slotting.ts`. Three values, and the difference between the last two is load-bearing — `midday` (a meal) and `sunset` (a stop whose value is the last light, timed off `sun/solar.ts` so December anchors to 4:42 p.m. and June to 8:23) are **reserved before the day is filled**, so two seeded ranger programs move the day around them instead of walking lunch to 5 p.m.; `evening` (the meal on the way out) is only a **floor** placed last, because reserving a December dinner would sit it in front of the five-hour Wapama hike and push the hike off the day. `dayPart` is opt-in and narrow: a stop that is merely *better* early (Mirror Lake's reflection) carries none — that is the drive order's job. `apps/guide/scripts/check-itineraries.ts` (`npm --prefix scripts run check`, or `run itineraries:check`) seeds and slots every preset across five dates through the app's own modules, and fails on a lunch outside midday, a sunset stop that misses sunset, an entry the capacity filter drops, or an anchored stop pushed off the day. Midwinter is exempt from the everything-must-fit rule only (three and a half fewer hours of light); anchored stops are never exempt.
- **Archive notes on stops (July 2026).** A Stop may carry an optional `history`: one short, sourced note from the Nature Notes archive (see the Nature Notes archive bullet in the root `CLAUDE.md`) with the citing issue's `volume`, `number`, and printed `issueDate`. `content/archive.ts` builds the link from those three values (`/archive/<year>/vol-<v>-no-<n>/` on the **editorial** origin, year taken off the end of `issueDate`), and `components/ArchiveNote.tsx` renders it under the body on the full read only (`StopCard` with `compact={false}`: the stop page and `/preview`) — a citation block on every card in a region list turns the list into a bibliography. Two rules, both load-bearing: **every fact in the note must come from the cited issue** (the citation is the whole point, and a reader who follows the link has to find what the note claims), and the link is an external one that will not open offline, which is why the note itself is bundled and only the source needs the network. `scripts/check-archive-citations.mjs` (wired into `npm --prefix scripts run check`) scans the seed files and fails if a citation points at an archive page that does not exist or that does not print the date the note claims — nothing at runtime can catch either.
- **Coords are web-verified, not ground-truthed.** A July 2026 pass checked every `coord` in `stops.ts` against NPS place/trailhead pages, USGS GNIS, and OSM-derived sources; each coord line carries its source and any prior offset. Stops still marked `// TODO: verify on the ground` (unsigned pullouts, off-trail features, conflicting sources) have no authoritative source; never strip those markers without a real field visit, because the field guide's value prop depends on accurate parking turnouts.

The storefront is indexable through **prerendered files**: after `vite build`, `scripts/prerender-public.ts` (the last step of `npm run build`) loads `src/prerender/entry.tsx` through Vite's SSR loader, renders each page in `lib/publicPages.ts` with the real `Preview`/`StopTeaser` components signed out, and writes `dist/preview.html` and `dist/stop/<id>.html` (its own title, description, canonical and social tags on `guide.thetalusfieldjournal.com`, `index, follow`), plus `dist/sitemap.xml` and `dist/robots.txt`. Six rules. (1) `publicPages()` is the one table: the routes take their document titles from it, so the crawler's rendered title matches the static one. (2) **Indexed: `/preview`, every core stop's teaser, and the five sample entries; not indexed: the Secret Guide's other teasers** (secret spots and hidden stops), whose names are the premium section's premise; their links still render as teasers from `index.html`. (3) **There is no `_redirects` file**, on purpose: Pages applies a `_redirects` rewrite even over a matching file, so `/* /index.html 200` would shadow every prerendered page; Pages' own SPA default (no top-level `404.html`) answers every path with no file with `index.html`, which is the same fallback. Never add a `404.html` to `public/`. (4) The static markup sits in `#prerender`, ahead of `#root`; `DropPrerender` in App.tsx removes it in the commit that puts the real page on screen (it shares the routes' Suspense boundary, so never while the fallback shows), and `main.tsx` removes it at once for a signed-in buyer. (5) The files carry `<meta name="tfg-prerendered">`, and `sw.js` never keeps such a page as the offline shell, or a buyer's offline launch would open on a stranger's landing page. (6) The pages.dev hosts are `X-Robots-Tag: noindex` by host rules in `public/_headers`, so the magic-link host never competes with the custom domain. `check-build-determinism.mjs` builds with the prerender step too.

- **The 3D map (September 2026; README.md, "The 3D map").** The basemap is a Protomaps vector build of OSM on Terrarium terrain (USGS 3DEP), both self-hosted as PMTiles in R2 and served one tile per URL by the Worker's `/vt/<ver>/…` and `/dem/<ver>/…`, which replaced the Esri World Topo raster (bulk offline caching of Esri tiles needs an ArcGIS licence a commercial app did not have). Five rules. (1) **Every colour is in `map/theme.ts`**, and the ones that are app tokens are read from `tokens.css` when the style is built, so the map follows daylight/granite; a scheme change rebuilds the style through `keepRuntimeLayers` in Map.tsx, which carries the page's own sources and layers across `setStyle`, so a runtime layer added later needs nothing extra, but a layer that must survive must be added with `addLayer`, never baked into `buildMapStyle`. (2) **Terrain and hillshade are two raster-dem sources over the same tiles**, as MapLibre recommends; merging them renders the hillshade at the terrain mesh's coarse resolution. (3) **The tile version is a path segment**, stamped into `sw.js` as `__MAP_TILES_VERSION__` by vite.config.ts; activate deletes other versions' tiles and the retired Esri `/tiles/` entries from `tfg-tiles`. Rebuilding tiles means `npm run map:tiles`, uploading both archives to R2, and only then merging the regenerated `map/tiles.generated.ts`. (4) **Offline is an overview pack plus four area packs** (`map/regions.ts`); an area `requires` the overview, which `useDownloads` fetches first and refuses to delete under a downloaded area, and pack sizes are measured by the generator (`regions.test.ts` fails when a box changes without a regeneration). The label glyphs and sprites (`public/map-assets/`) ride the overview pack and are served by the **tile** handler in `sw.js`, not the runtime one, because a pack fills exactly one cache. (5) **The opening camera is the Valley in 3D looking east** (`HOME_CAMERA`); the pin fit's `lastFitKeyRef` starts at `'all'` so the first unfiltered render does not flatten it into a fit-everything frame. Pins stay DOM markers (focusable `role=button`, placed on the terrain by MapLibre); label collision is the basemap's symbol layers'. (6) **Pins declutter in screen space (`map/declutter.ts`, pure and unit-tested; the pass is the declutter effect in Map.tsx).** Every camera move, coalesced to one pass a frame, places the pins in `KIND_STYLES[kind].rank` order (destinations, then orientation, then infrastructure; a Secret Guide entry half a step under its kind) and a pin whose box would land on one already placed steps down to a 12 px dot in its kind's colour (`map-pin--dot`); a tilted view also shrinks pins with depth (`depthScale`, the `--pin-scale` property the pin's CSS box reads) and stacks them nearer-over-farther by `z-index`. The open popup's pin, a planned pin and a focused pin are always drawn; a pin MapLibre marks as behind a ridge gives way; a pin drawn last frame keeps a 0.3 edge so equal ranks do not trade places while the map turns. It **hides, never drops**: a dot is the same element, in the tab order with its label, Enter opens its popup (and `:focus-visible` draws it full-size), and a tap on a dot zooms in toward it (`wirePin`, the one place a pin's click and keydown are wired). Everything is a class toggle and two style properties, so a pan never rebuilds a marker, and `.map-page__map` is its own stacking context (`z-index: 0`) so the pins' depth z-indexes never lift one over a pane; the popup sits above them at `z-index: 40000`. (7) **Every programmatic frame goes through `frameBounds`**: `overlayPadding` measures whatever floats over the map (the controls row, a side pane, the info card, a bottom sheet) and pads for it, the tilt drops to `DAY_FIT_PITCH` in 3D, and the reader's bearing is kept, because a bare `fitBounds` snaps the map north-up. A single place ("show me this one") uses `FOCUS_ZOOM` / `FOCUS_PITCH` (14 at 50 degrees): the elevation tiles stop at z13, and past about 14.5 a tilted camera draws overzoomed terrain with every near line ballooned. A selected pin lands `popupOffset` below the centre so its popup opens into the top half. The panes and the info card start below the view-controls row and right of MapLibre's zoom column on wide screens (`top: 66px; left: 58px`), and the itineraries list is a bottom sheet on phones, so opening one never covers the controls that move the map. The map's other data files (`public/map/roads-<hash>.json`, `public/map/trails-<hash>.json`) are named in `map/mapData.generated.ts` and stamped into `sw.js` as `__MAP_DATA_URLS__`, which activate uses to purge superseded copies. (8) **Nothing on a per-frame path may read the GPU back or the DOM's layout (the September 30 smoothness pass).** Profiled in headless Chromium on a laptop GPU, the 3D view ran at 9 fps during a pan and rotate (2.8 fps at 4x CPU throttle), and after the pass at 35 (13): the frame is MapLibre's to spend, and three library defaults and two of our own reads were spending it. (a) MapLibre fades a DOM marker behind a ridge by `gl.readPixels` on the terrain depth buffer, per marker, every 100 ms of motion, 41% of main-thread time with ~280 pins; pins are `PinMarker` (`map/pinMarker.ts`), which queues that test and drains it a few pins a frame once the camera rests. (b) `ScaleControl` unprojects two points every `move`, a coordinate-buffer readback each with terrain on; `SettledScaleControl` (`map/scaleControl.ts`) updates on moveend in 3D. (c) `Terrain.getElevationForLngLat` computes the whole covering-tile set per call to pick one zoom, and every pin calls it every frame through `project`; `map/terrainElevation.ts` wraps each Terrain the map creates so that zoom is computed once per frame through the public `map.coveringTiles`, with the same options. (d) The declutter pass reads the pin's position from `marker._pos` (MapLibre already projected it this frame), the canvas size from `map.transform`, and its own last-written styles from a JS cache; `canvas.clientWidth` there forced a style and layout pass over every freshly moved pin. (e) Nothing that floats over the map carries `backdrop-filter`, and the pin's contact shadow is a gradient, not a blur, because the compositor re-blurs a pane's whole footprint every frame the canvas under it changes. Terrain is in the **opening style** when the view opens in 3D (`buildMapStyle(theme, terrain)`), not set on `load`, where the first view drew flat and then heaved up under its pins, and the 2D/3D switch animates `terrain.exaggeration` with the tilt (`map/terrainToggle.ts`), removing terrain only once it is flat. (a) and (c) lean on MapLibre members typed in its d.ts but undocumented (`_updateOpacity`, `_pos`, `Terrain.getElevationForLngLat`); after a MapLibre upgrade, re-run a profile of a pan in 3D before trusting either, since a renamed member fails soft to the slow path rather than breaking.
- **The trip layer (the map's My trip tab, September 2026; README.md, "The 3D map").** The plan drawn day by day on the map, with `map/TripPanel.tsx` as its text alternative (every time, leg, distance and warning in list semantics). It adds **no trip logic**: `map/tripLayer.ts` reads `slotPlan` for times, `driveMinutesBetween` for leg times, `dayLegs` for late arrivals and `daylightFit` for hikes that end after dark, and adds only geometry, so the map, the board, `/today` and the print view cannot disagree. Six rules. (1) **Legs have a travel mode** (`travelMode` on every item type, the leg to the next item: `drive` / `walk` / `shuttle`; unset = `legMode`'s inference, a walk under 0.25 mi, else a drive), and slotting prices it: an **unset mode prices exactly as before modes existed** (legs.test.ts holds that), walk at 2.5 mph over 1.3x the straight line, shuttle by `trip/shuttle.ts` (walk to the nearest numbered Valleywide stop, five minutes a stop at the published 90-minute loop, plus a labelled 10-minute wait allowance; out of reach of a stop at either end it is priced as the drive). The board, the print view and `/today` name a leg by `legNoun`. (2) **A place is a custom entry, not a new item type**: `placeId` (`amenity:<id>` or `dining:<id>`, `trip/places.ts`) links a parking lot, campground, lodge or restaurant, whose name and coordinate are read live from the record, and `coord` pins one the reader placed. A new item type would have failed an older build's parse on a synced device; two optional fields are stripped instead. (3) **Driving and walking lines follow `public/map/roads-<hash>.json`**, the OSM road and path graph from `npm run map:data` (one-way rules kept: Northside and Southside Drive are one-way, so the Lodge to Curry Village routes round by El Capitan), routed by A* on the device in `map/roadGraph.ts`; the graph is content-hashed, rides the map overview pack, and is served cache-first from `tfg-tiles` with superseded copies purged on activate (`__MAP_DATA_URLS__`). It is ODbL and says so in its header. A leg the graph cannot route is drawn straight, faded, and flagged in the panel; the time is unaffected. (4) **Pins are map-drawn symbols**, shape per stop type (circle stop, diamond hike at its trailhead, square program, hexagon parking; `map/tripIcons.ts`), a number for the order in the day, colour for the day (`--day-1`..`--day-7` in tokens.css, Okabe-Ito without yellow), so colour is never the only signal; images are re-drawn on `styleimagemissing` after a scheme rebuild. In trip view the browse pins (DOM markers) are hidden, since they would sit over the trip pins and take their taps. (5) **The end-of-day warning is sunset by default** and judges only daytime blocks (hikes, stops not anchored to `sunset`/`evening`); a fixed end the reader picks judges everything. (6) Framing a day uses `frameBounds` (`DAY_FIT_PITCH`, 35, padded for the panel by `overlayPadding`): at the opening camera's 60 degrees Glacier Point's ridge hides the Valley's pins. In trip view the trails layer and the pin filter bar are hidden too: the plan draws its own hikes, and the chips do not narrow its pins.

- **The bearing instrument (`/compass`, August 2026).** Pick any coord-carrying stop (the picker is `guideStopGroups()` filtered to coords, so it and the log index the guide identically) and the SVG rose points at it: live straight-line distance and true bearing from a `watchPosition` fix, the device's compass heading, GPS elevation, and the sun's azimuth on the same dial (`sun/position.ts`, the NOAA position series evaluated at the actual instant — solar.ts's noon-fixed evaluation would put the marker a quarter degree off by evening; verified against the NOAA calculator to under a degree). Everything is computed on-device, which is why the page can promise airplane mode. Four decisions are load-bearing. (1) **Sensors report magnetic north and every printed bearing is true**: `useHeading` adds the park's declination (12.9° E) before anyone sees a number, and the footnote says so. (2) **A heading the platform cannot vouch for is ignored**: iOS `webkitCompassHeading` and Android's `deviceorientationabsolute` alpha are the only accepted sources — a relative alpha is whatever direction the phone pointed at page load — and silence past three seconds resolves to a north-up rose whose copy says to steer by the number, the ParkNowPanel honesty rule applied to sensors. (3) The hook smooths on the unit circle (EMA over sin/cos, raw angles break at the 359→0 wrap) and exports `continuousDeg`, the heading unwrapped along the shortest arc **in the event path**, because the CSS-transitioned rose must never spin the 350° way around and render is not allowed to touch refs (the react-hooks compiler lint enforces this; don't move the unwrap back into the component). (4) Starting is a button, not an effect: iOS orientation permission only resolves inside a user gesture, and a page that throws two permission prompts on open reads as malware. The default target is the stop nearest the session's **first** fix (`useGeoWatch.firstFix`, never replaced), derived state rather than an effect write, so the target cannot silently switch mid-walk; `?to=` mirrors with replaceState (the /search idiom) and stop pages deep-link it via the Bearing action. The cardinal letters ride the card radially like a real compass card — keeping them upright would need a counter-rotation that cannot stay in sync with the transitioned rose.
- **The Help card (`/help`, September 2026).** The one screen for the moment something has gone wrong, and the page a buyer opens once. Until it existed the numbers a person needs in trouble were spread across three essentials essays and nothing in the guide could read the reader's own position back to them. Top to bottom: `tel:911` and `sms:911` (a text needs less signal than a call, and the copy says so), the live fix in **both** dialects a dispatcher and a rescue team use (decimal degrees, latitude first, and degrees/decimal-minutes with hemisphere letters), its accuracy and GPS elevation, "where that is" (the nearest named place with distance and the bearing *from the place to the fix*, so the sentence reads "0.3 mi NE of Olmsted Point" and is true as spoken), Share/Copy of one plain-text message ending in a maps link (`lib/position.ts`, unit-tested; an SMS often gets out where a call will not), a four-line script for the call, the park's own numbers as one-tap rows, and where a person or a landline is. Entry points: the sixth instrument tile on Home (which closed the grid evenly and retired the wide fifth tile), the `safety-and-help` essential, the compass footnote, the bottom nav's Guide group, and a manifest shortcut so a long-press on the home-screen icon reaches it.

- **Cross-device sync salvages, and salvage never writes a blank.** `sync/schema.ts` re-validates every pulled document because one written by a newer build is exactly the case that must not corrupt this one, and it repairs rather than rejects: a document whose plan went bad still carries usable favorites, visited marks, and notes. The rule that makes that safe is that a field the parse could **not** read is flagged in `unparseable` and then **skipped** by `applyRemote`, never written. The repaired document carries an empty placeholder so the envelope parses, and applying a placeholder over real data is a wipe: it took out every saved stop, visited mark, and note on the device, and then the heal push carried the loss to every other device (CODE-AUDIT-2026-08 §2). Three parts hold it up. (1) A list survives only **intact** — filtering out the entries that fail and keeping the rest is the same wipe in slow motion, since a newer build storing `[{id, addedAt}]` filters down to zero strings. (2) An **absent** field counts as unparseable, not as "the other device has none": every build that has written this document writes all four. The plan is the one exception, because `null` is a valid plan. (3) The heal push (`canHeal` in `planSync.ts`) fires only when **nothing** was lost, for the mirror reason: pushing our version of a field we could not read destroys the newer build's copy and re-clobbers it every pass. It used to guard the plan alone, which is how the other three were left exposed.

- **Service worker** (`public/sw.js`) caches the app shell + photos for offline use. **Version-coherence invariant (August 2026):** the navigation handler re-caches fetched HTML under the running worker's `SHELL_CACHE`, but only after `shellAssetsCached()` confirms every `/assets/*` URL that HTML references is already in some cache — right after a deploy the OLD worker fetches the NEW build's HTML, and storing that against the old chunk set was a white screen on the next offline launch. The asset handler's counterpart: on a miss in its own shell cache it tries `caches.match()` across all caches before the network (hashed URLs are content-addressed, so a cross-version hit is always correct). Install retries each fetch once and deletes its partial cache on failure, so one dropped connection on park wifi no longer scraps a whole update. The `UpdateBanner` component prompts when a new build is available; the "an update is waiting" fact is **latched in `pwa/registerSW.ts` module state** (`getPendingUpdate`/`onUpdateReady`), not delivered as a one-shot event, because an update found during boot fires before the banner subscribes and a remount used to drop it — both read as "the update bar disappeared". `registerSW` also reads `registration.installing` directly (the spec fires `updatefound` *before* `register()` resolves, so a listener attached in the `.then` misses the first one), keys "is this an update" off `registration.active` rather than `navigator.serviceWorker.controller` (a hard reload loads the page uncontrolled), and re-announces a still-waiting worker after every poll/focus `update()` — which is what brings the bar back if it was ever lost. **Hard invariant since the July 2026 audit:** an HTML response (the SPA 200 fallback: Pages answers a path with no file with `index.html`) must never be cached under an asset/photo/tile/shell key — the SW and `useDownloads.fetchIntoCache` both guard with an `isHtml` check and treat such a response as a failed URL, and `activate` self-heals by purging HTML from the runtime and tile caches. Photo pre-warming goes through `pwa/precache.ts` (`PRECACHE_URLS` message, prod-only, works pre-controller via `serviceWorker.ready` with a 10 s timeout). The `beforeinstallprompt` event is captured at module level in `pwa/installPrompt.ts`, called from `main.tsx` **before render** (component listeners fire too late); `Welcome.tsx` and `InstallPrompt.tsx` consume it via `useDeferredInstallPrompt`. **iOS has no such event and never has**, so nothing can make Safari show an OS install dialog: the ask there is the app's own `components/InstallSheet.tsx`, which gives per-browser Add to Home Screen steps (`iosBrowser()` in `utils/platform.ts` tells Safari from Chrome/Firefox/Edge from an embedded webview, where installing is impossible and the only useful instruction is "open this in Safari"). `lib/install.ts` owns the two flags and `shouldAutoPrompt()`, whose claim is memoized in module state because a `useState` initializer runs twice under StrictMode and the banner remounts on every route change — either would burn the one unprompted open. - **Nothing in the build may read the clock (August 2026).** The Pages project rebuilds on every push to `main`, and most of those pushes change nothing in this app — the nightly `chore: record nightly Lighthouse run [skip ci]` commit is the usual one, and `[skip ci]` is a GitHub Actions convention that Cloudflare Pages does not honor. So any wall-clock value that reaches `sw.js` or a hashed chunk turns every no-op redeploy into a real update for every installed copy: a rotated shell cache, a full re-download over park LTE, and an "Updated. Tap to refresh." bar for a build with no changes in it. Two did, and both are fixed: the SW cache name was a full ISO timestamp (so `sw.js` differed on *every* build, verified as two builds fifteen seconds apart), and `VITE_BUILD_DATE` was `define`d into the bundle, where it landed in the main chunk and the Account chunk and moved their content hashes at every midnight. The SW version is now a **content hash** of the SW template, the API base, and the sorted asset filenames — which is exactly the question the browser asks when it byte-compares `sw.js`, so a real change (app code, CSS, the SW itself) still fires the banner, same-day hotfixes included, and an unchanged rebuild correctly announces nothing.

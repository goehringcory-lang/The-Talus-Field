The short version: the Field Guide, the paid companion app to this site, is
free for a month if you are on this list. The code is TALUS30 and the door is
at the bottom of this letter.

I built the guide for the part of a trip this site cannot reach: the part
where you are standing in the park and the signal is gone. It is a web app.
It lives at guide.thetalusfieldjournal.com, installs to your home screen if
you want it there, and downloads itself to your phone, so it keeps working at
a trailhead with no bars.

What is in it:

- **The park, stop by stop.** 66 places across the Valley, Glacier Point and
  Mariposa Grove, Tuolumne, and Hetch Hetchy: where to park, when to go, and
  what you are looking at when you get there.
- **Every day hike in the park.** All 57, with verified distance and gain, an
  elevation profile for each, and a GPX file if you carry a GPS app.
- **A trip board.** Drag stops onto days. It orders each day by drive time,
  keeps lunch at midday, and holds a sunset stop at that evening's actual
  sunset, whether that is 4:42 in December or 8:23 in June.
- **Live readings.** Valley forecast, today's light, road status, entrance
  waits. A reading that goes stale disappears rather than showing you an old
  number, because a wrong reading sends somebody up a closed road.
- **The Secret Guide.** The unsigned pullouts and quiet corners that do not
  go in print.
- **The rest.** Ranger and partner programs matched to your dates, the night
  sky, where to eat, a wildlife quick-ID guide, and find-it lists for the
  kids.

The code: go to
**guide.thetalusfieldjournal.com/redeem?code=TALUS30**
and type your email. Access arrives in your inbox as a link and a six-digit
code, the same way a purchase does. No card, nothing to cancel. The code
works once per email address and runs 30 days from the day you use it. If
you already own the guide, redeeming changes nothing; it just re-sends the
access you have.

After the month it simply ends. If it earned its place on your phone, the
full guide is $3.99 for 18 months.

Why now: Tioga Road is open, the high country is in its best stretch of the
year, and once the Labor Day weekend clears out, September is the quietest
good month the park has. A month is enough to plan a fall trip and walk it.

See you out there.

Cory

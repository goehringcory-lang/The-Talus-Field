# Voice guide

The Talus Field sounds like a naturalist who lives in the park explaining it to a friend: dry, declarative, warm underneath. Target reading level is 8th to 10th grade. This guide sits on top of the house style in CLAUDE.md (no exclamation marks, no em-dashes in reader-facing copy).

## Principles

1. **Say the useful thing first.** Lead with the answer, the number or the decision. Context follows.
2. **Plain words, short sentences.** Use the shorter word when meanings match. Vary length, but cut any sentence that needs two readings. (NPS interpretive writing standards and federal plain-language guidance both start here.)
3. **Explain the science once, in passing.** If a technical term earns its place, define it in the same sentence. Never lean on it.
4. **Concrete beats abstract.** A named trail, a time of day or a mileage does more than "stunning" or "iconic." Save superlatives so they count.
5. **Active voice, a person doing something.** "The park closes the road" over "the road is closed by the park."
6. **Welcome the first-timer, respect the regular.** Never assume prior knowledge and never talk down. One clause of explanation costs a veteran nothing.
7. **Make it a connection, not a lecture.** NAI's principle: tie the place to what the reader already cares about, then let the resource make the point.
8. **Keep the author's personality.** Dry humor and a firm opinion are the brand. Polish the line, don't sand it flat.

## Do / don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Name the place, the number, the month | "Breathtaking," "must-see," "hidden gem" |
| Use "use," "start," "enough" | "utilize," "commence," "sufficient" |
| Define a term on first use | Assume the reader knows "talus," "headway," "recreate" |
| Give the CTA a clear verb and a result | "Learn more," "Click here" |
| Say what you don't know | Claim a condition the site has not read |
| Change wording only | Change facts, figures, dates, names, quotes, URLs |

## Before and after

- **Before:** Dropping the reservation system fails the park on both halves of its mission: this Memorial Day weekend, visitors couldn't recreate and the meadows took the damage.
  **After:** ...visitors couldn't find parking, and the meadows took the damage.
  *"Recreate" is agency jargon; the body says the same thing with the parking detail.*
- **Before:** Check it the morning you drive in, not the week before: Yosemite changes faster than a booking window.
  **After:** Check it the morning you drive in, not the week before. Roads and crowds change faster than your plans do.
  *Drops the abstract "booking window" and names what actually changes.*
- **Before:** Know what's open, what's booking out, and what's worth your time. The Sunday Letter brings the view from inside the park to your inbox, once a week.
  **After:** Know what's open, what's booking up, and what's worth your time. One letter a week, from inside the park.
  *The heading already names the letter, so the second sentence can be shorter.*

Nature Garden a New Feature of Yosemite Museum Ranger-Naturalist Enid Michael Throughout the spring and summer months visitors to Yosemite have expressed surprise and pleasure at the change in the area directly back of the Yosemite Museum of Natural History. This area is a part of the alluvial fan spread at the mouth of Indian Canyon. It is in the warmest section of the valley and there are present here, in the native flora, many plants from the Sonoran Zone, or foothill section. The planting of this garden was begun last April. FAHA Field School, 1932 While standing in front of the apparent effort, that one Yosemite Museum I heard a very scarcely see the throbbing of the soft, musical song coming from throat. The jay continued to hop within the foliage of a large black oak and search around for several minutes and during this interval he and endeavored to locate its source. After a careful search only a bluefronted jay was to be seen. -A bit of speculation, however, told me that this beautiful song could not come from the throat of a jay. We are all accustomed to hearing a coarse blatant squawking or a series of harsh staccato notes issuing from this bird. The name itself suggests to us a noisy, saucy, individual and one is not disappointed in this variety. However, as I saw this bluefront hopping about with great agility in the tree, I continued to hear these soft notes. Finally, the jay came out of the tree and foraged on the ground. Then only did I know that the noisy jay was the author of his whisper song. It was given so very low, and without was always in song. Apparently this soft vocal expression is a message of some sort, possibly a love song to the mate.

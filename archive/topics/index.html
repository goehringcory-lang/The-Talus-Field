The Nature Notes archive

Topics in Yosemite Nature Notes, 1922–1954

6 subjects from the bulletin's own pages: short passages quoted as printed, each linked to the issue it came from, and the headlines it ran on each subject. For everything else, browse the whole run by year.

  1. Black bears

    10 passages · 46 headlines · 1922–1953

    Many visitors to the Bear Pits have been well rewarded this year. At dusk or after dark is the time when they are usually seen, but this season they are even to be seen in broad daylight and are becoming very tame

    July 10, 1922 · Vol. 1, No. 1

  2. Mule deer

    8 passages · 31 headlines · 1922–1948

    Mule deer are often to be seen near the public camps. A fawn, lost from its mother, was recently taken to the Rangers' Clubhouse where it has found a good home. It should be remembered that this is the only species of deer found in the Park.

    July 17, 1922 · Vol. 1, No. 2

  3. Giant sequoias

    9 passages · 33 headlines · 1922–1954

    The cross section of a wind felled Giant Sequoia which is on exhibition at the Museum is daily proving of great interest to visitors. The section is 9 feet in diameter. It was cut 40 feet from the base of a 14-foot tree.

    July 10, 1922 · Vol. 1, No. 1

  4. Glaciers

    9 passages · 23 headlines · 1923–1954

    On the north side of Mt. Lyell there is to be seen a cirque yet occupied by a living glacier, the snout of the once mighty ice tongue that played a part in excavating the Hetch Hetchy.

    July 1924 · Vol. 3, No. 12

  5. Tuolumne Meadows

    9 passages · 13 headlines · 1922–1954

    Last year one was even seen in the Sierra Club Camp near Soda Springs and this year one has been observed just above Tuolumne Meadows Lodge. This rabbit turns white during the winter and develops snow shoes like the snowshoe rabbit.

    July 24, 1922 · Vol. 1, No. 3

  6. The water ouzel

    10 passages · 11 headlines · 1922–1952

    The Nature Guides have been leading crowds every Monday morning to see a Water Ouzel's nest which is located on Tenaya Creek about forty yards beyond the Iron Spring.

    July 10, 1922 · Vol. 1, No. 1

Sunday Field Notes

The bulletin ran until the 1980s. The habit behind it did not stop: a short note from inside the park on Sundays, when there is something to say.

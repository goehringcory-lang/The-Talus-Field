In the bulletin's words

  1. Many visitors to the Bear Pits have been well rewarded this year. At dusk or after dark is the time when they are usually seen, but this season they are even to be seen in broad daylight and are becoming very tame

    July 10, 1922 · Vol. 1, No. 1

  2. Bears have been so numerous this past summer that few people have left the park without seeing a real live bear.

    August 28, 1923 · Vol. 2, No. 12

  3. Old timers in the Yosemite declare that there has never been a time when bears were so numerous in the Valley.

    1923 · Vol. 2, No. 14

  4. For example, few records have been made of observations on hibernating bears in a truly wild state; at any rate, such records pertaining to certain individual animals, with accurate dates, have been difficult to obtain.

    January 1925 · Vol. 4, No. 1

  5. Our most recent game laws have legalized the slaughter of Black Bears the year round. Now let all loyal Californians unite their voices in one insistent demand that the last of our bears be given adequate protection.

    February 1928 · Vol. 7, No. 2

  6. The trapping of park bears in a humane manner is accomplished through the use of a large, galvanized-iron cylinder, mounted on trailer wheels.

    November 1938 · Vol. 17, No. 11

  7. A female American Black Bear (Ursus Americanus) gave birth to four cubs.

    October 1939 · Vol. 18, No. 10

  8. During the summer of 1939, while on duty at Glacier Point, I had an exceptionally fine opportunity to observe these animals, for during that season the garbage truck hauled its load to the disposal pit each morning between 7 and 8 a.m.

    August 1942 · Vol. 21, No. 8

  9. It has been estimated that Yosemite Valley is able to support only about three or four bears on their natural forage.

    May 1952 · Vol. 31, No. 5

  10. Bear trees are those whose trunks exhibit claw marks; these are usually long, deep, and vertical, but occasionally may be diagonal or horizontal.

    August 1953 · Vol. 32, No. 8

Sunday Field Notes

The bulletin ran until the 1980s. The habit behind it did not stop: a short note from inside the park on Sundays, when there is something to say.

59 entries in the Field Guide quote an issue from this archive at the place it describes. The archive is the reading; the guide is the walking, offline, at the trailhead where there is no signal.

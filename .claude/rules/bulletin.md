# The Park Bulletin (/now) and its edition data

Moved from the root CLAUDE.md (Editorial site conventions); loads when a matching file is touched.

- **"The Park Bulletin" (`/now`)** is the one-page everything-happening board (it replaced the weekly "This Week in the Park" dispatch in July 2026): the current NPS Yosemite Guide edition condensed into headline tiles, a dated change ledger, a day-by-day program schedule, road/area status, trail status, hours, transit, and phone numbers. Content lives in `bulletin.json`, rewritten once per Guide edition (~every 5 weeks; its `__comment` is the field reference and the workflow), rendered by `page-now.jsx` (`BulletinPage`; own `?v=` counter on `BULLETIN_URL`, shared with `TIOGA_BULLETIN_URL` in `page-tioga-opening.jsx` (the homepage's `HOME_BULLETIN_URL` went with the September 2026 redesign), like the map's `POINTS_URL`), served to non-JS crawlers via the `HUB_PROSE` entry in `edge/seo.js`, and its sitemap lastmod tracks `edition.updated` automatically. **The September 2026 redesign moved the date arithmetic off the reader and into the page.** It used to print the edition the way the Guide prints it: ten alert paragraphs, a program clock whose rows carried their own day codes and date bounds in the title ("from Sep 4", "Su W F"), three prose cards for the rest of the park, and a calendar half of which had already happened. Every answer was on the page and the reader had to work out which ones still applied. Now `headlines` (two to four editor-written tiles) opens the page; `changes` is one dated ledger the page splits at **today in the park** into "Coming up" and "Already closed" (a past `closes`/`ends` row drops into the closed list, a past `hours` row into "Hours changed", a past `opens`/`event` row disappears because the schedule already carries it); `programs` merges the old `valleyDay`, `elsewhere` and `events` into one structured list the reader filters to a single day and area, with the Guide's free, all-ages and accessible marks as filters; `hours` rows carry `closes` / `then` so a row reads as it is today ("Closed", "8 – 9 since Sep 7"). Five things are load-bearing. (1) **Today is the park's day, not the reader's** (`btParkToday`, America/Los_Angeles): a visitor planning from Berlin at breakfast wants Yosemite's date. (2) **Every helper in `page-now.jsx` carries a `bt` / `BT_` prefix.** Page bundles are classic scripts sharing one global scope, and the first build of the redesign named one `shortDate`, which `page-dates.js` also declares; the schedule crashed on the reader's first interaction, when the prefetch landed. A generic top-level name in a page file is a collision waiting for the next page. (3) **`scripts/check-bulletin.mjs` (in `run check`, `run bulletin:check` alone) is the only thing that can see the new failure mode.** A day code of `thu` for `th`, an `until` before its `from`, or a year typo does not break the page, it silently removes rows from it, and a program that never appears reads as "nothing is on". The check proves every row renders at least once inside the edition window, that no edition day and no area tab is empty, that every retired key is gone, and that no em-dash reached reader-facing copy. (4) **The marks are the printed Guide's own symbols**, `access` / `allAges`, read off its Programs page row by row and **true-only** — an unmarked program is one the Guide did not mark, not one it ruled out, which the schedule's note says in as many words. The same two ride the PWA's program feed as `accessible` / `familyFriendly` in `workers/src/data/manual-programs.ts`; re-read both every edition. (5) **Status colour has its own three tokens** (`--bt-open` / `--bt-warn` / `--bt-closed` in `styles.css`): the chips used to draw "open" in `--moss` and "closed" in `--rust`, which were the same iron red in the retired Golden Hour palette, so an open road and a closed trail differed only by fill. **The icon layer (August 2026)** is the page's wayfinding and its rule is unchanged: `BULLETIN_ICONS` in `page-now.jsx` is the registry, the ordered `[pattern, icon]` tables under it resolve a row's mark from its own name, and `styles.css`'s `.bicon` supplies fill, stroke and weight from `currentColor` so the geometry carries no colour and every palette follows. A mark may only restate what the JSON already says (the chips read `tone`, the program marks read the Guide's symbols, an area reads its own name, a headline reads its optional editor-set `icon`), an unmatched name falls through to the neutral `dot` rather than guessing, and a mark that is the only carrier of its meaning gets a label. Icons are drawn to survive 16px: the first pass shipped a trail curve that rendered as a plain slash and a tent that read as the warning triangle beside it, so redraw and look at the page before believing a path. **The fold (August 2026, kept).** Hours, transit, know-before-you-go and the full phone list are standing reference and sit under four `BulletinFold` `<details>`, closed, with the first four `numbers` pinned above them; the fold **hides, it never drops**, so every row stays in the DOM and the crawler prose is unaffected, and a summary's hint is built by `hintFrom` from the rows' own names rather than hand-written copy that could drift. Adding a section means deciding which half it belongs to, and edition-specific is the higher bar: the page earns its length by answering "what is different", not by being complete. It is a standing editorial commitment: carrying a stale edition past its end date without a note is worse than none, which is what `edition.notice` and the automatic ended-edition banner are for.

# /map, the trip hand-off to the Field Guide, /itineraries and /conditions

Moved from the root CLAUDE.md (Editorial site conventions); loads when a matching file is touched.

- **Map page** (`page-map.jsx`, `/map` route): Google Maps JS API trip builder over `points.geojson`. The API key sits in `index.html`, domain-restricted in the Google Cloud console; `mapId` is required by AdvancedMarkerElement. Pins are maintained **by hand** in `points.geojson` (it was seeded once from the PWA's stops via `scripts/seed-points-from-stops.mjs`; re-running overwrites the file). The trip persists in localStorage (`tfg.trip`) and is shareable via `/map?trip=id1,id2`. The **whole map** is newsletter-gated (`tfg.map.unlocked`): `MapAccessGate` covers the page over a blurred live map until one Buttondown signup (or any prior signup on the site) unlocks it; bypassable by design and fails open when storage is unavailable. Overlay controls (`Reset view`, `Find me` geolocation with a "you are here" dot) sit bottom-left of the map. A July 2026 UX pass added category filter chips (`?cat=` param, `CATEGORY_STYLES`), stop search, marker clustering (MarkerClusterer lazily injected from unpkg below zoom 13, fails soft to plain markers; unpkg is CSP-allow-listed), a peek/half/full mobile bottom sheet, and undoable trip-change toasts. Quick-pick itineraries are derived from `window.ITINERARIES`, and a "Email this trip to yourself" box (`TripEmailBox`) submits to Buttondown **and** POSTs `{email, stops, website}` to the Worker's `/api/trip/email`, which builds the share URL server-side and mails it via Resend. **The first-visit layer (September 2026)** sits at the top of the sidebar (`MapSetup`): the reader picks an entrance (`ORIENT_GATES`) and a month, persisted in `tfg.map.setup` and seeded from the trip selector's `when` answer. The month's road status is read from `window.TRIP_MONTHS` (so `/map` now loads `intent-data.js` through `PAGE_MODULES`), never restated, and drives a dashed Tioga Road / Glacier Point Road overlay (OpenStreetMap geometry in `ROAD_LINES`, attributed in the on-map key), a warning on trip stops behind a closed road (`REGION_ROAD`), and a line in each affected InfoWindow. Gates, gas, the two visitor centers and the area drive-time labels are markers with their own layer chips; area labels show only at zoom 10 to 11 and marker text hides below 10, because a phone's first frame piles them up. Every line of that copy restates a published article, named in the comment beside it: the articles say there is no gas at Tuolumne, so the gas layer has none there.
- **Trip hand-off to the Field Guide.** The map sidebar's "Open this trip in the Field Guide" links to `<app>/trip?import=<ids>`. The two catalogs overlap but are not identical (`points.geojson` was seeded once from the guide's stops, and several editorial pins match a HIKE id rather than a stop id), so `apps/guide/src/trip/importTrip.ts` resolves alias → stop → hike and **reports what it could not bring across rather than guessing** — a wrong turnout is the failure this product exists to avoid. `scripts/check-trip-bridge.mjs` (in `npm --prefix scripts run check`, `run bridge:check` for the per-pin breakdown) is what keeps that promise honest, because nothing else measures the bridge: the only prior guard lived behind `import.meta.env.DEV` inside `importTrip.ts`, a `console.error` in a browser nobody has open, checking stale aliases alone. It fails on an alias whose target no longer resolves (which degrades to "isn't in the guide", reading to a buyer as missing content rather than a broken table) and on an alias keyed on an id the guide already carries (alias lookup runs first, so it would silently redirect a real entry). It **warns**, rather than fails, on a pin with no counterpart and no `EDITORIAL_ONLY_LABELS` entry — a pin the guide does not carry is legitimate, but an undeclared one falls back to its raw id, so the reader is told "the-fen didn't come across" — and on stale rows in either table. It prints the coverage rate (24/31 as of Aug 2026), which is the number nobody had. Ids are also stashed at PWA boot (`tfg.trip.pendingImport`, 30-day TTL) because a reader who does not own the guide yet leaves for Stripe and returns through `/open?token=`, which knows nothing about the trip; Home then offers it, and only an explicit tap writes to the plan.
- **Itineraries + Conditions pages** (July 2026). `itineraries-data.js` is a raw (uncompiled) script like `data.js`: it defines `window.ITINERARIES` (four plans: `1day`/`2day`/`3day`/`halfday`, each day listing `points.geojson` ids in drive order) and `window.getItineraryStopIds(id)`; it is the single source of truth for both `/itineraries` (`page-itineraries.jsx`, which fetches `window.POINTS_URL` to resolve stop names and links each plan to a real `/map?trip=…` navigation) and the map's quick picks. `edge/seo.js` carries a **hand-maintained** TouristTrip ItemList for `/itineraries` — keep it in sync when plans change. **`/conditions` (`page-conditions.jsx`) is the station board (September 2026 redesign).** It was a single 680px column of link-outs; it is now a full-width board whose top-left is the answer (the `.readout` ink plate: today's date plus one line per live feed) and whose body is one section per reading. Three shared components carry the live layer, each mounted here with an opt-in **`variant="board"`** that leaves their default markup alone for the other surfaces that mount them (`EntranceWaits` → `.gates`, also on `/webcams`; `ParkingNow` → `.lots`, also on `/now`; `WebcamStrip` → `.cam-grid--board`, also on `/webcams` and `/firefall`) — a new treatment goes behind a variant, never by editing the default. Since the September 2026 design rollout the page sits on the homepage design system (see the homepage bullet): the readout is the aside of an `HpPageHead`, each reading is a numbered design section, and the two asks are the shared `HpGuideBand` (still `location: conditions`) and an `HpLetter` (still tag `alert-roads`); `.cond-head` and `.cond-asks` no longer render. The page still fetches nothing itself: the two feed components hand it a digest through an optional **`onData`** prop, which is what fills the readout without a second request per feed (the digest effect's deps must stay normalized numbers; `fetchedAt` is `NaN` while a feed is silent and `NaN !== NaN` re-fires forever). Four rules hold the page up. (1) **Nothing on it asserts a condition the site has not read**: the only live sources are the NPS waits and lot feeds, both silent rather than wrong, and everything else is a standing fact (an elevation, a gate's highway, the recorded-line number) or a link to whoever does know — which is why the roads block lists three sources instead of printing statuses, since a hardcoded "Tioga Road: open" is correct for about six months a year. The seasonal kit section (`CondSeasonKit`, keyed to the park's month in America/Los_Angeles, like `/stay`'s season note) is not an exception: what a month calls for is a standing fact the season articles already publish, not a reading, so it never names today's weather. (2) **No gauge has a fill meter**: both feeds publish a value without a maximum (a wait in minutes, a lot's free spaces), so a bar would draw a ratio the data does not contain. (3) The gate board has **three** cards because the NPS feed publishes three gates; Tioga Pass is absent on purpose, as a fourth card that could never fill reads as a broken sensor. Tone thresholds are the feed's own (`waitClass`: ≤5 short, ≤15 moderate, above that long), and on the inverted plate the alarm colours are lifted (`--rust` fails contrast on near-black). (4) The forecast stations sit on the NPS park map (the shared `.npsmap` overlay, `ConditionsElevation`): three numbered pins at their real coordinates and a Tioga Pass marker, with the heights printed on the cards, never drawn. An earlier version drew terrain between the stations; no drawn terrain belongs on the page. The API Worker's `/api/weather` could fill those cards with live highs and lows, but its four spots are the Field Guide's regions and adopting them would drop Wawona from this page, which is an editorial call.

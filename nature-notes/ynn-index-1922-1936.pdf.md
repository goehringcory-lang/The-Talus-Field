FOREWORD
The contents of this volume appeared first as se parate preprints for use in the respective national parks. For this reason,
and also because several indexes were in process simultaneously, it
was impossible to page the volume continuously. The indexing was
done by numerous workers -- a circumstance which. may account for
some inconsistencies and variation in choice of subject headings.
Moreover, the work was done in part indirectly from the annotated card catalogue of "Nature Notes" which had previously been
made for the division of Public Relations at National Park Service
headquarters, Wasnington, D.C. The indexers were therefore largely
dependent upon the accuracy and judgment of the annotators in assigning subject headings.
A word about the arrangement of the index may be helpful to
its users. Emphasis throughout is on the subject aspect, although
an attempt was made to list every author whose identity was known.
The only exceptions in either case were trivia such as holiday
greetings or brief announcements of only passing interest.
Personal names used as subjects are distinguished from authors
by having the Christian names spelled out. In some cases it was
necessary, in order to reduce the bulk of the assembled volume, to
omit contributors' names in the citation under subject headings.
They appear, however, in the usual fullness as author entries.
The compiler has not attempted any systematic classification
of flora and fauna, although such topics as Animals, Geology, and
others have been loosely grouped together for convenience. When
there were only a few references to a subject, they have been placed
without differentiation in the general alphabet. Common names have
been preferred to scientific, even when the latter form was used in
the original article.
The "NATURE NOTES" of Grand Teton and Shenandoah national
parks, having originated in 1935 and 1936 respectively, are not
rep resented because of their slight extent.
Grateful acknowledgment is made to the Works Progress Administration, which furnished labor and part of the materials for this
undertaking, and to the Civilian Conservation Corps, which supplied
a typist to cut a considerable portion of the stencils.

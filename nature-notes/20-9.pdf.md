NATURE NOTELET
At the edge of the Museum Wildflower Garden early on the morning
of May 28, 1941, Associate Park Naturalist Beatty and I watched an
adult Sierra Chickaree transporting
its young. Although the young
chickaree was nearly one-third as
large as the parent, little difficulty
was displayed by the mother as she
swiftly ran along through the tall
grass. The young one had the appearance of a little fur ball as it
clung to the breast and throat of its
mother. They both were last seen as
the mother ran across the road west
of the museum and disappeared in
the thickets beyond.
—Willis A. Evans

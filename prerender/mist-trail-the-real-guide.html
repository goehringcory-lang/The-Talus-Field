The Mist Trail is the most hiked trail in Yosemite, and it might be the most hiked trail in any national park. On a Saturday in June, a thousand people walk some version of it. On Reddit it gets more questions than every other Yosemite trail put together: Is it hard? How wet will I get? Can my kids do it? What shoes do I need? Is it worth it? Can I die?

The short answers, in order: yes, soaked, probably, not sneakers, absolutely, and statistically unlikely but not impossible.

Here's the rest, from someone who has hiked it more times than he can count and has watched thousands of people try it in everything from perfect sunshine to sideways rain.

What the Mist Trail actually is

The Mist Trail starts at Happy Isles, at the east end of Yosemite Valley, and climbs beside two waterfalls: Vernal Fall (317 feet) and Nevada Fall (594 feet). The full trail to the top of Nevada Fall is about 5.4 miles round trip with roughly 2,000 feet of elevation gain. If you only go to the Vernal Fall footbridge, where most families turn around, it's 1.6 miles round trip with about 400 feet of gain.

You can stop at any of four natural turnarounds, and each one works as a full hike on its own.

The Vernal Fall footbridge (0.8 miles from Happy Isles, 400 ft gain). Paved trail, a moderate grade, and a clear look at Vernal Fall from the bridge. Most families with young kids turn around here, and it's worth the walk even if you go no farther.

The top of Vernal Fall (1.3 miles from Happy Isles, 1,000 ft gain). This is where the trail earns the "mist" in its name. You climb a granite staircase right next to the fall, and when it's running at peak volume (May and June), you get drenched. Not sprinkled. Drenched. The granite steps are wet, steep, and uneven. A metal railing is bolted into the rock on the exposed side. At the top there's an emerald pool above the fall and a view down the canyon. It's hard work, and it's a thrill.

The top of Nevada Fall (2.7 miles from Happy Isles, 2,000 ft gain). Past Vernal, the trail heads through forest and up the Merced River canyon to the base of Nevada Fall, then to its top. Up there you're looking down 594 feet of whitewater with Liberty Cap and Half Dome standing behind you, one of the most dramatic views in the park.

The loop return via the John Muir Trail (total ~6.5 miles). From the top of Nevada Fall you can come back down the JMT, which switchbacks more gently through the forest. You get a loop, and your knees get a break on the way down. Most experienced hikers go up the Mist Trail and down the JMT.

Keep going past Nevada Fall and you're on the standard approach to Half Dome. If that's the plan, read the permit lottery guide first, and the honest case for the cables before you commit.

The wet granite reality

From mid-April to late June, the granite staircase below Vernal Fall is basically a waterfall of its own. Spray hits the trail the whole time. The steps, hundreds of them, carved and placed by the Civilian Conservation Corps in the 1930s, are wet, mossy in places, and uneven. Some are a foot tall. Some are two feet tall. Some are angled in ways that make your feet slide.

This is where most Mist Trail injuries happen. Not people falling off the trail: people slipping on wet granite stairs in the wrong shoes.

Shoes matter on this trail more than any other trail in Yosemite. Hiking boots or trail shoes with real tread are the minimum. Running shoes with worn-out soles are a slip waiting to happen. Flip-flops, sandals, and fashion sneakers are genuinely dangerous on wet granite, and I say that without exaggeration. I've watched people go down hard on these stairs in shoes that had no business being on a wet rock face.

If you don't own hiking shoes and you're coming in peak waterfall season, buy a cheap pair with rubber lugs. Thirty dollars is a lot less than spending your Yosemite trip in the medical clinic.

You will get wet

This isn't a gentle misting. In May and June, when the Merced River is running at full snowmelt volume, the spray zone below Vernal Fall feels like standing in a rainstorm for twenty minutes. Your clothes get soaked through. So does your phone, your camera and your pack.

Here's how to deal with it.

Put your phone in a ziplock bag. You can still shoot photos through it. A waterproof phone case is even better.

Wear synthetic or wool, not cotton. Wet cotton gets heavy and cold and takes hours to dry. Synthetic dries in about thirty minutes in the sun at the top of the fall.

A rain jacket helps, but you can skip it on a warm day. In July and August, when the falls are lower, you might get misted rather than soaked. In May and June it keeps you warm on the way up, though you'll sweat under it.

Pack a dry shirt in a ziplock and change at the top. That one move is the difference between a great day and shivering the rest of the way.

When to go

Peak waterfall (May through mid-June). The big show. Vernal Fall is thundering, the mist zone is intense, the granite is soaked, and Nevada Fall is at full power. This is the version people come for. It's also the most crowded and the most slippery.

Mid-season (late June through July). The flow drops, the mist zone shrinks, and the stairs get drier and safer. Still beautiful, and the weekday crowds thin a little.

Late season (August through October). The falls slow to a trickle, the trail is bone dry and the stairs are easy. It feels more like a canyon hike than a waterfall hike, but the granite alone makes it worth doing.

Time of day. Go early. Happy Isles fills by 8 a.m. on summer weekends. The closest parking is the trailhead lot at Curry Village, pinned on the site's map. Early on, the trail is in shade, so you hike cooler and get better light on the falls. By afternoon it's in full sun, and the heat coming off the granite stairs is brutal.

2026 weekday restrictions. Starting June 30, 2026, the Mist Trail between Vernal Fall and Nevada Fall is closed Monday through Thursday, 7 a.m. to 3:30 p.m., for trail repairs, through the end of October. On those days, either time it so you reach that section after 3:30 or take the JMT bypass between the two falls. Weekends aren't affected. And the bypass got better this summer: the JMT's "Ice Cut" segment, between Clark Point and the Panorama Trail junction, reopened in late July 2026 after nearly three years closed for storm and ice damage, so the full Mist Trail-JMT loop is open again.

The safety talk

I'll be blunt here, because the internet makes this trail look like a walk in the park, and it isn't one.

In 2025, roughly 30% of Yosemite's search-and-rescue operations originated on the Mist Trail corridor. Thirty percent, from one trail. The park averages close to one fatality a month on this trail and the falls above it. That's not a scare tactic. It's just the number.

Most of the deaths here are cardiac arrests, people who weren't ready for how strenuous this hike is. The effort is real, the climb doesn't let up, and summer heat makes both worse. If you have a heart condition, or you don't hike much, take that seriously before you start.

The pools above Vernal Fall and Nevada Fall look calm and inviting. They aren't. The current is powerful, the granite is frictionless when wet, and the lip of the fall is just downstream. There is no second chance. If you go past the railing, you are in the kill zone. The NPS signs aren't being dramatic. They're being accurate.

Stay behind the railings. Don't wade. Don't swim in the pools above the falls. Don't walk on the wet granite near the edge "for a photo." This is the single most important safety rule on this trail, and it's the rule that, when broken, has killed people. Swimming in the Emerald Pool and the Silver Apron isn't just a bad idea, it's prohibited; the swimming guide lays out where in this park you may and may not get in the water, and what the Park Service has documented happening here.

The trail itself hurts a lot of people too. Not fatally, but with twisted ankles, broken wrists, heat exhaustion, and falls on wet granite that require a ranger assist or a helicopter. That 30% SAR number isn't only about the falls. It's people underestimating the trail, running out of water, wearing the wrong shoes, and getting in over their heads on steep, wet rock. This trail is beautiful and it is dangerous, and those two things are true at the same time.

Setting the falls aside, the trail is steep and physical. People with heart conditions, serious knee issues, or no hiking experience should consider turning around at the footbridge. The staircase to the top of Vernal Fall gains 600 feet in half a mile on uneven wet stairs. You'll feel it.

Bring more water than you think you need. The trail starts at 4,000 feet and gains 2,000 feet in full sun on the upper sections. Two liters minimum. Three liters if you're going to the top of Nevada Fall. There's no water on the trail itself. Water and restrooms are at Happy Isles at the start and at the top of Nevada Fall (seasonal).

The crowd question

Yes, it's crowded, especially on weekends from May through July. The Mist Trail is Yosemite's signature hike and it draws people from all over the world.

The good news: the crowd thins fast as you climb. The footbridge is packed. The staircase is busy. The top of Vernal Fall has people. The section between Vernal and Nevada Falls is noticeably quieter. The top of Nevada Fall, on a weekday, can feel almost empty.

Want the Mist Trail without the crowd? Go on a weekday, start before 7 a.m., and hike past Vernal Fall to Nevada Fall. Most people turn around at Vernal, so the ones who keep going get a bigger waterfall and a fraction of the people.

Is it worth it

Yes, no qualifiers. The Mist Trail in peak waterfall season is one of the great hiking experiences in the United States. The granite canyon, the thundering water, the work of the stairs and the view from the top add up to something you won't find anywhere else.

Just don't show up in flip-flops with one bottle of water and no idea what you're walking into. Online, it looks like a casual stroll past a pretty waterfall. It isn't. It's a real hike in a real mountain environment, and the people who prepare for it have a great day. The people who don't are the ones writing the Reddit posts about how they slipped on the stairs, ran out of water, and wished they'd stayed at the hotel.

So come ready. Read the day pack packing list, and pack the car the night before so the early start actually happens. Start early. Wear real shoes. Bring a ziplock for your phone and a dry shirt for the top.

Then plan on getting soaked. The Mist Trail earned its name, and it's worth every wet, steep, breathless step.

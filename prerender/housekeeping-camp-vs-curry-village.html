Housekeeping Camp and Curry Village sit a short way apart on the east end of the Valley floor. Both put canvas over most of their guests and a steel bear locker beside them, both are run by the same concessioner, and both book on the same website. They are not the same kind of night.

Housekeeping Camp is a seasonal river camp where you bring your own bedding and cook dinner over your own fire. Curry Village is a village of tent cabins, cabins and motel rooms with made-up beds, a pizza deck, a dining hall and a grocery, open most of the year. Pick by how you want to eat, what month you are coming, and how much of camping you actually want to do.

The short version

  • Cooking your own, with a fire and a river beach: Housekeeping Camp.
  • A made-up bed and someone else's pizza: Curry Village.
  • Mid-October to March: Curry Village. Housekeeping Camp is closed.
  • At either one, everything with a scent goes in the bear locker.

SIDE BY SIDE

Two canvas roofs, two different trips

Both are on the Valley floor's east end, on the free shuttle loop. Housekeeping Camp is on the bank of the Merced River, west of Curry Village, at shuttle stop 12. Curry Village sits against the south wall, below Glacier Point, at stops 14 and 19, and the Mist Trail starts one stop on, at Happy Isles. The YARTS bus from Merced stops at Curry Village.

Neither is a campground. The Park Service's 13 campgrounds book through Recreation.gov and are a separate system; the camping guide covers those, and the campground reservations guide covers the 7 am release on the 15th. These two are concessioner lodging, which means a roof, a bed and a front desk, and a price that moves with the date.

HOUSEKEEPING CAMP

Half camping, on the river

Housekeeping Camp is 266 units, each a three-sided concrete structure with a canvas roof and a privacy curtain across the open side. Inside there is a set of twin bunks and a double bed, a table, chairs and a mirror, electric lights and outlets. The covered patio has a fire ring with a grill grate, and each unit has two bear lockers of different sizes. A unit sleeps up to six with two cots added.

You bring your own bedding, or rent a bed pack: $15 a night for a double (two blankets, two sheets, two pillows), $9 for a single. You cook your own meals over the fire ring, and wood fires are allowed from 5 to 10 p.m. The concessioner calls these the only accommodations in Yosemite where outdoor cooking is permitted. The Merced's sandy beaches are at the edge of the camp for the hot afternoons, and the place feels like a family summer camp.

It is austere. The walls are concrete, the curtain is a curtain, there is no door to lock, and the neighbors are close. The camp has a laundry room, a guests-only shower house with soap and towels, and a small store with groceries and camp supplies. It suits people who want to half-camp: camping's campfire and cooler, with a bed frame and no tent to pitch.

CURRY VILLAGE

A village of canvas under Glacier Point

Curry Village has taken guests at the base of Glacier Point since 1899, when David and Jennie Curry, two former schoolteachers, founded it. Today it is mostly canvas tent cabins (canvas walls and roof, real beds, no plumbing), with wood cabins that have their own bath and electric heat, and the Stoneman rooms, motel rooms with private bathrooms and one to three double beds.

The heat question decides the tent. An unheated tent has electric light and no outlet, and it is cold in spring and fall. A heated tent has one outlet, and the concessioner runs the heat from the Tuesday after Labor Day to the Friday before Memorial Day. The shower house is open around the clock, Wi-Fi is in the guest lounge rather than the tents, and quiet hours run 10 p.m. to 6 a.m., because canvas carries every sound in both directions.

Around the tents is the rest of the village: the kitchens, a pool from Memorial Day to Labor Day, a bike stand from spring to fall, the Mountain Shop, and the Yosemite Mountaineering School's guided hikes and climbing classes (the bike stand and the school both closed for 2026 on October 25). In winter there is an outdoor ice rink, which the current Yosemite Guide lists as opening November 13, 2026, if conditions allow.

The name. From 2016 to 2019 the place was called Half Dome Village, during the trademark dispute with the park's former concessioner that also renamed the Ahwahnee. It has been Curry Village again since 2019, but some older pages and photo captions still carry the other name. They are the same place. And the village stops where it does for a reason: after rockfalls off Glacier Point on October 7 and 8, 2008, the Park Service permanently closed 233 units under the cliff, about a third of what the village offered visitors. THE FOOD

Your own fire, or the pizza deck

At Housekeeping Camp you are the kitchen. Bring a cooler, a pan and the food for the trip, and cook over the fire ring between 5 and 10 p.m. The camp store sells groceries while the camp is open, and the Village Store in Yosemite Village carries the park's largest selection of groceries. The kitchens at Curry Village are an easy walk or a shuttle ride when nobody wants to cook.

Curry Village is the other way round. There is no cooking at the tents, and the compound's kitchens feed you instead: four on the current Guide's list, and a grocery. The Pizza Deck is the obvious stop after a hike, and the Coffee Corner opens early enough for a trail start, which saves the drive to Yosemite Village for coffee. Hours shrink after the summer, so read them against the date you arrive; the current Guide's are beside this paragraph. The Meadow Grill taqueria and Jennie's Ice Cream are summer counters, and the current edition lists both as closed for the season.

THE BEARS

The locker is not optional at either

Canvas is not a barrier a bear respects, and concrete with a curtain is not much better. The Park Service requires every Housekeeping Camp guest and every Curry Village tent-cabin guest to keep all food in the lockers provided, and never to keep food, drinks, toiletries or trash in a tent or tent cabin. To a bear, food includes trash, recycling, toiletries, soap, unwashed dishes and anything else with a scent, and coolers count even when empty. Guests in Curry's hard-sided cabins and Stoneman rooms may keep food inside the room.

The car is not the answer. After dark, nothing with a scent stays in a vehicle, trunk included, and by day food, drinks and coolers stay hidden from sight with the windows closed. A violation can bring a fine of up to $5,000. Housekeeping Camp asks more of you here, because you arrive with a cooler and a week of groceries; plan where it all goes before you unload. The bear guide lists what counts as food and why the rule exists.

  • In the locker

    All food and drinks, coolers even when empty, trash, recycling, toiletries, soap, unwashed dishes.

  • Not in the car

    Nothing with a scent after dark, and nothing in view by day.

  • Every time

    Close and latch it whenever you leave the unit, even for a few minutes.

  • Up to $5,000

    The fine for a storage violation, with food or a car liable to be impounded.

WHAT IT COSTS

Priced by the night you search

This piece does not print nightly rates, because the concessioner posts no standing price for either place: the booking page shows the price for the night and room type you search. What it does post: occupancy over two adults costs extra at Curry Village ($17.50 per added person 13 and older in a tent, $14 in a cabin, $9.75 in a Stoneman room, plus tax), Housekeeping Camp's bed packs are $15 and $9 a night, and lodging tax and assessments add about 13.7 percent to the room (a 12 percent occupancy tax, a 1.5 percent tourism district assessment and a 0.2 percent state tourism fee).

Count the whole trip, not the night. A Housekeeping Camp family that cooks its own meals spends very little on food once the cooler is packed. A Curry Village family eats at the kitchens, which is the convenience it is paying for. The trip-cost breakdown runs the full numbers for a week.

WHO SHOULD BOOK WHICH

Pick by how you want to eat

Book Housekeeping Camp if

  • You are a family or a group of up to six who would rather cook than queue.
  • You want a campfire and a river beach, and no tent to pitch.
  • You could not get a campground reservation and want the next thing to it.
  • Your trip falls between early April and mid-October, by 2026's dates.

Book Curry Village if

  • You want a made-up bed and someone else to make dinner.
  • You are hiking, and the room is a place to sleep between trails.
  • You are coming in late fall or winter: book a heated tent, or a cabin.
  • You want a private bathroom: a cabin with bath or a Stoneman room.

Two warnings, one for each. Curry Village is not a quiet hotel at a discount: the canvas is thin and the village is busy, and it works best for families and hikers who treat the tent as a place to sleep. Housekeeping Camp is not a cabin: it is concrete, canvas and a curtain, and it rewards the visitor who arrives packed like a camper. If you are first-time campers, the first-time camping guide is a good test of whether you want the fire ring. Neither place takes pets beyond service animals; the pets guide has what does.

If both are full, rooms come back. The Park Service's advice is to check the concessioner's site every morning. If nothing turns up, the lodging page has the gateway towns and their drive times.

What is the difference between Housekeeping Camp and Curry Village?

Housekeeping Camp is 266 three-sided concrete units with canvas roofs on the Merced River, each with a set of twin bunks, a double bed, a covered patio, a fire ring and bear lockers; you bring bedding and cook your own meals. Curry Village is canvas tent cabins, wood cabins with bath and Stoneman motel rooms with made-up beds, shared bathrooms for the tents, and its own kitchens and grocery in the compound. Housekeeping Camp is open spring to fall only; Curry Village runs most of the year.

Is Housekeeping Camp open in winter?

No. It runs spring to fall, and its 2026 season was April 3 to October 12. Its store closed with the camp on October 12, 2026. In winter, Curry Village's heated tent cabins are the nearest equivalent on the Valley floor.

Can you cook at Housekeeping Camp?

Yes. Each unit has a fire ring with a grill grate, and wood fires are allowed from 5 to 10 p.m. The concessioner says its units are the only accommodations in Yosemite where outdoor cooking is permitted. Curry Village has no cooking at the units; you eat at its kitchens or buy food at its grocery.

Do you need to bring bedding to Housekeeping Camp?

Yes, or rent a bed pack: $15 a night for a double (two blankets, two sheets, two pillows) and $9 for a single.

What food is there at Curry Village?

The September 23 to November 24, 2026 Yosemite Guide lists four kitchens and a store: the Seven Tents Pavilion for breakfast and dinner, the Pizza Deck, the Coffee Corner, Bar 1899 and the Gift & Grocery. The Meadow Grill taqueria and Jennie's Ice Cream are summer counters, listed as closed for the season. Hours change through the year; the current Yosemite Guide prints them.

Was Curry Village called Half Dome Village?

Yes, from 2016 to 2019, during the trademark dispute with the park's former concessioner that also renamed the Ahwahnee. It has been Curry Village again since 2019, though some older pages and photos still use the other name.

How do you book Housekeeping Camp or Curry Village?

Both are run by the park concessioner, Yosemite Hospitality, and book through travelyosemite.com. Reservations open 366 days ahead. The first night is charged at booking and refunded if you cancel at least seven days before arrival; Housekeeping Camp keeps a $15 fee on those cancellations.

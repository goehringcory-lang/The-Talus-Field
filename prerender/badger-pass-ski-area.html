Badger Pass is a small ski hill inside a national park, and it is small on purpose: ten runs, five lifts, an 800-foot vertical drop, and a third of the terrain rated for beginners. Nobody flies in for it. People come because it is a gentle place to put a child on skis for the first time, inside a national park, because the tubing hill needs no skill at all, and because the parking lot is the trailhead for the winter rim of Yosemite Valley. The question that matters more than any of that is the first one people search: is it open. The honest answer is that it depends on the snow more than the calendar, and the last two winters prove it.

This is the operating guide: the season and how to check it, the drive and the shuttle, the downhill area, lessons, tubing, the Nordic center and the ski track to Glacier Point, and the huts. The season-wide picture, including Dewey Point and the chain-control explainer, is in the winter guide.

The name, briefly

The ski area is the Badger Pass Ski Area, which is what the concessioner and the Park Service both call it. Some maps, apps, and older articles still use Yosemite Ski & Snowboard Area, the name the park gave it in 2016 during a trademark dispute with Delaware North, a former concessioner that claimed the rights to Badger Pass and other historic names in the park. The dispute was settled in July 2019 and the original names came back. It is one place under two names; search for either.

It is also an old place. The Park Service calls Badger Pass the oldest downhill skiing area in California, and says the West's first ski lift, an "Upski," was built there in 1933 and the historic lodge opened in 1935. The concessioner dates its ski school to 1928.

Is Badger Pass open? The season, and how to check

The published season is mid-December to mid-March, conditions permitting, with the lifts running daily from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. The Park Service gives the downhill season as mid-December through March, conditions permitting, and plows Glacier Point Road as far as the ski area from mid-December through early April.

"Conditions permitting" is the load-bearing phrase. In February 2025 the concessioner and the Park Service announced that Badger Pass would not open at all for the 2024 to 2025 season: the snow had come too late, they said, to make a short season worthwhile, and season passes were refunded. The next winter it opened late and closed early. The Nordic center, which runs the cross-country and snowshoe trails, opened on Friday, January 9, 2026, the lifts followed on January 16, and the concessioner closed the area for the season on March 18, 2026, after a week of unseasonable warmth melted the snow. Neither winter came close to mid-December.

The 2026 to 2027 opening date has not been announced as of this writing, and the concessioner's pages still carry last season's notices. The tubing hill in particular is built only from natural snow, so for much of what Badger offers, the date is set by the storms, not by a schedule. Three ways to check, in order:

  • The Snow Phone, 209/372-1000, the concessioner's line for conditions, weather, and ski area information.
  • The concessioner's Badger Pass pages at travelyosemite.com, which post the opening and closing notices.
  • The park's road recording, 209/372-0200, then 1, then 1, for whether Badger Pass Road is open and under chain control that morning. The conditions page collects the park's live feeds in one place.

Getting there: the road, the chains, the shuttle

Badger Pass sits on Glacier Point Road, which the park calls Badger Pass Road in winter, five miles east of its junction with the Wawona Road (Highway 41), at a base elevation of 7,200 feet. The concessioner puts it about 45 minutes' drive from Yosemite Valley. The road is one of the three the park says need chains more often than the rest, along with the Wawona Road and the Big Oak Flat Road. Whenever a chain control is in effect, every vehicle must carry chains or cables, four-wheel drives and rentals included, and failing to put them on when required carries a citation of up to $5,000.

The alternative is the free Badger Pass shuttle from the Valley, which the park says runs whenever the ski area's facilities are open, typically mid-December through March. The concessioner says it runs daily, subject to weather and road conditions, and links a printed schedule; the version it posts at this writing is dated January 2024, so confirm the times in the Valley before you count on them. One detail the concessioner does spell out: the morning shuttle reaches Badger Pass at about 9:30 a.m., and the guides on its 9 a.m. snowshoe hikes will not wait for it. For the drive in from outside the park, the entrances guide compares the routes.

Downhill: the hill, the tickets

The concessioner's figures: 7,200 to 8,000 feet, an 800-foot vertical drop, ten runs rated 35 percent beginner, 50 percent intermediate, and 15 percent advanced, served by five lifts: one triple chair, three double chairs, and a handle tow. There is a terrain park. If you measure a ski area by steepness, this is not one to travel for. If you measure it by how gently it introduces a seven-year-old to a chairlift, it is close to ideal.

The ticket prices below are the ones the concessioner posts at this writing. It has not published a 2026 to 2027 price list, so treat these as last season's figures and the floor of what to budget.

  • All day, all lifts: $79 adult, $59.50 child (7 to 12).
  • Half day, all lifts: $72 adult, $46.50 child.
  • Lower lifts only (the Turtle handle tow and the Bruin chair): $35 adult, $17.50 child.
  • Children 6 and under ski free with a paying adult, and skiers 70 and older get one free lift ticket with ID.
  • A one-ride ticket, $6.50, is sold for access to the cross-country trails, for riders carrying skis or snowshoes.

All-day tickets are sold online; half-day and lower-lift tickets are sold at the ski area. Residents within 50 miles of the park get 25 percent off Monday through Friday, sold at the day lodge. Rentals run $44.50 for an adult all-day ski package (skis, boots, and poles), $42 for an adult snowboard, and $10.50 for a helmet.

Lessons: where Badger earns its reputation

The ski school is the reason most families come, and its packages are built for people who have never been on snow.

  • Guaranteed Learn to Ski or Snowboard: $154 adult, $140 child (7 to 12). Rental, helmet, a beginner lift ticket for the Turtle handle tow and Bruin chair, and two group lessons, morning and afternoon. The concessioner's guarantee is that you ride the beginner run on your first day, or the return lesson is free. Arrive an hour and a half early for rentals.
  • Introduction to Downhill Skiing or Snowboarding: $130 adult, $110 child, with one group lesson at 9 a.m., 11:15 a.m., or 2:15 p.m., first come, first served. The 2:15 lesson is $110 adult and $95 child.
  • Badger Pups, ages 4 to 6: $100 for one lesson or $130 for two, with rental, helmet, and a beginner lift ticket.
  • Private lessons: $140 an hour for one person, rising to $440 an hour for seven. Adaptive lessons are arranged by phone at 209/372-8430.

The minimum age for the group packages is 7. As with the tickets, these are the prices posted at this writing, not a published 2026 to 2027 list.

Tubing: natural snow only

Sledding is not allowed at Badger Pass. What there is instead is a tubing hill, and it is the most snow-dependent thing on the mountain: the concessioner gives its anticipated operating dates as January through March, weather permitting, because the hill is built entirely from natural snow and no snowmaking can open it early. Two sessions run a day, 11 a.m. to 1 p.m. and 1:30 to 3:30 p.m., at $30 per person for two hours. Riders must be at least 42 inches tall. There is no conveyor belt, so you carry the tube back up. Tickets are nonrefundable within seven days of the session, and tubes not claimed before a session starts are resold.

For free sledding, the park's snow play area is at Crane Flat, on the Big Oak Flat Road, open in winter when conditions allow. Take broken sleds home with you; the park asks.

Cross-country: the Nordic center and the road to Glacier Point

The other half of Badger Pass is flat, quiet, and much larger. The concessioner lists 90 miles of marked trails and 25 miles of groomed track from its Nordic center, which is open daily from 8:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. in season. Beyond the ski area, the Park Service closes Glacier Point Road to cars and grooms it, in part, for skiing and snowshoeing, along with a short semi-loop to Old Badger summit. Restrooms are at Badger Pass, and a pit toilet stands at Summit Meadow, about a mile out. The park's etiquette is simple: snowshoers walk outside the parallel ski tracks, and the center lane is for skate skiers and beginners.

The marquee route is the road itself: 10.5 miles one way to Glacier Point, a 21-mile round trip that is a long day for a strong skier and an overnight for most people. The Glacier Point guide covers the summer version of the same road. Shorter, ungroomed routes lead to Dewey Point on the Valley rim, and the snowshoe routes from the same lot are in the snowshoeing guide.

Rentals and lessons come from the Nordic center. An adult classic package (skis, boots, and poles) is $36.50 all day or $29.50 half day; a two-hour introductory group lesson is $86 and runs daily at 10:15 a.m. and 1 p.m. with three or more students; a one-hour private lesson is $75 for one person plus $25 for each additional person. Guided custom tours start at $250 for a half day for one person and fall per head with group size. The Nordic center's number is 209/372-4996.

Sleeping out: the two huts

Two huts sit at the far end of the Badger Pass trail network, and neither is a casual plan.

The Glacier Point Ski Hut, run by the concessioner at the end of the 10.5-mile ski in, sleeps up to 20 and takes guests 14 and older with an adult. It did not open for the 2025 to 2026 season, which the concessioner attributed to a lack of power, and as of this writing it has posted nothing about 2026 to 2027.

The Ostrander Ski Hut, run by Yosemite Conservancy, is a stone hut at 8,500 feet, about 10 miles from Badger Pass with more than 2,500 feet of climbing, and the Conservancy describes it as for experienced skiers only. Its 2026 to 2027 season is listed as December 23, 2026, through April 4, 2027, subject to change. Space is awarded by a fall lottery: for 2026 to 2027, applications ran from October 12 through 11:59 p.m. on October 18, 2026, with winners notified by October 23, and beds left over are released first come, first served from 9 a.m. on December 1, 2026, and most Tuesdays after that. As of mid-October 2026 the trails south of Glacier Point Road toward Ostrander Lake were still closed for the Dome Fire, during which crews protected the hut; check the park's closures before you apply and again before you go. A free wilderness permit from the Badger Pass Ranger Station, the A-frame building at the ski area, is required.

Camping in the snow is the third option, also on a wilderness permit. The park requires winter campers to sit at least a mile from any plowed road and a mile and a half from the ski area boundary, and does not allow camping at Summit Meadow, Dewey Point, or Glacier Point themselves.

Where to stay for a Badger Pass trip

The in-park beds for a Badger Pass trip are in Yosemite Valley, about 45 minutes' drive or a shuttle ride away, because the Wawona Hotel is closed while the Park Service assesses the building's condition, with no reopening date posted. Valley lodging in winter runs at the concessioner's winter rates outside the holiday and firefall blackouts, and the trade-offs among the Ahwahnee, Yosemite Valley Lodge, and Curry Village are in the lodging guide. Outside the park, the gateway towns on Highway 41 sit on the road Badger Pass Road leaves from; the town-by-town comparison is on the lodging board.

The short version

Badger Pass is a beginner's hill and a cross-country trailhead with a lodge between them, and in a year with snow it is one of the best winter days in the park. The season is set by the storms: assume nothing before January, check the Snow Phone the night before, and carry chains even if you plan to take the shuttle. If the snow is late, the Valley still has the rink, Columbia Rock, and the walk into the Mariposa Grove. If the snow is good, come midweek in January or February, book the learn-to-ski package for the first-timer, and give the rest of the party the road to Glacier Point. By March the season can end in a week.

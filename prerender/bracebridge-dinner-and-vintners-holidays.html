Two names turn up every year in the same searches: the Bracebridge Dinner and Vintners' Holidays. Both happen at the Ahwahnee. Both get described online in the same breathless language, "iconic," "legendary," "bucket list." Neither description tells you what the evening actually is, what it costs, or whether the tickets you want are still there to be had. They are two different events, on two different calendars, sold two different ways, and conflating them is the fastest way to book the wrong one or miss the window on both. Here is what each one is, what it costs, when it sells out, and an honest read on whether it is worth the trip.

Two events, not one

Vintners' Holidays runs in November: four multi-day sessions built around California winemakers, each ending in a five-course paired dinner. A session includes a "Meet the Vintners" opening reception, four tasting seminars led by the visiting winemakers, and the closing gala dinner, sold as a package with a room at the Ahwahnee, Yosemite Valley Lodge, or Curry Village. You cannot buy just the tastings; the room is part of what you are booking.

The Bracebridge Dinner runs in December: a single, several-hours-long dinner theater pageant, staged repeatedly through the month in the Ahwahnee's Great Lounge and dining hall. It is Tudor-costumed, live-sung, built around a seven-course feast, and involves a cast and crew that runs past a hundred people. Unlike Vintners' Holidays, a Bracebridge ticket buys the performance and the meal only. A room at the Ahwahnee is a separate booking, and plenty of guests drive in for the evening and drive home after.

The overlap that confuses people: both are staged in the same building by the same concessioner, both are the kind of thing a magazine calls "a Yosemite Christmas tradition," and both get lumped into vague web copy that never says which one has a wine list and which one has a knight in it. They don't compete for the same night. They compete for the same kind of trip, the one built around a single expensive evening rather than a week of trails.

What the Bracebridge Dinner actually contains, if the name means nothing to you: a cast of characters (a pious parson, a French chef, a wild Scottish cousin, a lusty widow of several husbands, and a Lord of Misrule who runs the room) leads guests through a procession of courses, including a boar's head and a baron of beef carried in by costumed servants, some of the props original to the 1920s production. A professional chorale sings carols between courses, there's a wassail bowl, and the whole thing runs about four hours. It reads as more theater than dinner, because it is: you are watching a play while it feeds you, not attending a meal with background music.

Where both of these came from

The Bracebridge Dinner is nearly a century old. The Ahwahnee opened in 1927, and the hotel's then-president, Donald Tresidder, wanted an opening-season holiday spectacle to match it. He hired pageant director Garnet Holme to build one around Washington Irving's Old Christmas at Bracebridge Hall, and the first dinner ran that December. Holme died in 1929; the hotel handed the rewrite to a young photographer who had been playing the production's "Lord of Misrule," and Ansel Adams's version of the script is substantially the one still performed. For decades the dinner was one of the hardest tickets in California: through 2002, roughly 2,000 seats a year were allotted by lottery against as many as 60,000 applications. The concessioner has since added performances and moved to first-come, first-served sales, which makes the tickets easier to get and no less expensive.

Vintners' Holidays is younger and started smaller: a 1982 idea from the Ahwahnee's then-beverage manager and chief concierge to fill the hotel's slow weeks between the fall shoulder season and the December rush, built around a handful of visiting winemakers. It grew into a recurring, sponsor-driven program that now runs four sessions every November.

One historical wrinkle worth knowing if you read anything written between roughly 2016 and 2019: during that window, a trademark dispute with the park's former concessioner forced Yosemite Hospitality to rename the Ahwahnee "The Majestic Yosemite Hotel" and rebrand several of its programs. The dispute was resolved and the original names, including the Ahwahnee and the Bracebridge Dinner itself, came back into official use afterward. If you find an older review or brochure using the "Majestic" branding, it is describing the same place and the same dinner.

What each one costs, and what the money buys

Vintners' Holidays packages, room included, have started somewhere north of $450 per person based on double occupancy in recent years; the exact number moves with which room category and which session you pick, and rises from there for the Ahwahnee's better rooms. That price covers the reception, all four seminars, the closing gala dinner, and the room itself for the length of the session. Measured against a standalone hotel stay plus a nice dinner out, it is not an outlandish add, since you are paying for a room you would have booked anyway plus a genuinely unusual set of wine programming.

The Bracebridge Dinner is the pricier evening on a per-hour basis, because you are buying only the performance and the meal. Tickets have run into the several hundreds of dollars per adult for years, and after the dinner's return in December 2024 following a five-year pause, reporting on that season put prices over $500 a person, with lower-priced passes for children. That is dinner-and-a-show money at a scale most restaurants don't approach, and it buys exactly that: a few hours, not a room, not a second night, not a discount if you're already staying at the hotel.

Both events run through the same channel, travelyosemite.com or 888-413-8869, which is also the number the Field Guide's own Park Bulletin lists for park lodging generally. Neither ticket type shows up on the general lodging-availability tools that cover the gateway towns; they are inventory the concessioner controls directly.

What books out first

This is the practical question, and the two events answer it differently. Bracebridge Dinner tickets typically go on sale eight to nine months before the December performances, which for a given year lands around March or April, and they have sold out every year since the dinner's return. If you are reading this in late summer hoping to walk into a December seat, the honest answer is that the good dates, and often all of them, are usually gone by the time the leaves turn. The dinner also runs on a fixed schedule of specific performance nights across the month, roughly every other day from early to late December, so there is no such thing as "just show up on a slow Tuesday": every night is its own sold ticket allotment.

Vintners' Holidays lodging books on the same 366-day window as any other Ahwahnee, Lodge, or Curry Village reservation, one year and a day ahead, rolling. That means the November session dates open to booking roughly a year before each one runs, and the same logic the Field Guide has laid out for in-park lodging generally applies here without modification: set a reminder for the morning that window opens if your dates are fixed, and if you miss it, check back regularly. Cancellations happen continuously, with a real wave in the final weeks before any date, and a Vintners' Holidays package that shows sold out in July can have space again in September.

Put simply: if you want this December's Bracebridge Dinner and you haven't already booked it, you are likely working the cancellation list, not the original sale. If you want next December's, the move is to watch for tickets to go on sale in the spring and buy immediately. Vintners' Holidays gives you more runway either way, since it rides the same rolling booking window as a normal room, and the same patience-rewards-persistence rule applies.

What the park is actually like that week

Neither event happens in isolation from the season around it. Tioga Road and the upper stretch of Glacier Point Road are typically closed by November, chain requirements on the Valley approach roads are a real possibility on any given night, and the days are short: the sun is down well before five in December, and the drive out after a late Bracebridge performance is a dark, possibly icy, mountain drive. The winter guide covers what's open, what's closed, and how the chain rules actually work, and it is worth reading before you commit to a same-day drive-in-drive-out Bracebridge trip rather than staying the night. If dinner is the whole point of the visit, a room removes the single biggest risk in the plan, which is not the pageant, it's the road afterward.

Is it worth it

For the right trip, yes, with conditions attached. Both events are genuinely unusual, not manufactured hotel programming: Vintners' Holidays is real access to working winemakers in small-group seminars, and the Bracebridge Dinner is a nearly century-old theatrical production that exists nowhere else, with production values and a cast size that a $500 restaurant tasting menu cannot buy you. If the occasion justifies the spend, an anniversary, a milestone birthday, a family tradition you're starting, both deliver something a normal trip to this park does not.

They are not the way to introduce a family to Yosemite for the first time, and they are not a budget trip by any definition; a standard visit here costs a fraction of either package. The Bracebridge Dinner in particular is not built for young children: the performance runs about four hours, the dress code is genuinely formal (a dark suit or cocktail dress at a minimum, tuxedos and gowns are common, and costumes on guests are discouraged since the cast is already wearing them), and most guidance around the event treats it as unsuited to kids under ten. Vintners' Holidays, being a room package built around wine seminars, is even less of a kids' event by design. They also are not primarily hiking trips: both happen in the shortest, darkest weeks of the year, with most of the high country locked behind winter road closures, so the days around the event are for the Valley floor, not the granite above it. If what you actually want is a normal winter visit to this park, at a normal winter price, the season itself is the better article to read, and a standard room at any of the Valley's lodges gets you the empty trails and the clearing-storm photographs without the four-figure evening.

If the Ahwahnee itself is sold out for the dates you want, whether for a Vintners' Holidays package or simply a room to pair with a Bracebridge ticket, the gateway towns are the fallback, though none of them replicate the point of either event, which is being inside the building where it happens. A live availability search around the park is the fastest way to see what's actually left on your dates before you decide the trip doesn't work this year. For a bite that doesn't require a costume or a wine glass, the Ahwahnee's dining room also runs its regular seasonal menu on nights neither event is happening, covered along with the rest of the Valley's kitchens in the Field Guide's restaurant guide.

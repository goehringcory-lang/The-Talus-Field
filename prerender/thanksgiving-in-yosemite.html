Thanksgiving 2026 falls on Thursday, November 26, seven weeks from the date of this article. A four-day weekend in Yosemite is a sound plan for it, with a catch in the timing. November is one of the quieter months of the year in the park, and Thanksgiving week is the exception to it. The weekend also lands in the gap between seasons: the high roads have almost always closed by then, the ski area and the plowed road to it have not started, and one of the three Valley lodging properties closes on the Sunday the weekend ends. Plan for the park as it actually is that week, not as it is in October or January, and the trip works.

What follows is the weekend as the park's own records and the concessioner's published schedule describe it: the weather normals, what the road record says will be open, the holiday dinner, where to sleep, the campgrounds, and how to fill four short days. Everything dated here is for 2026 unless it says otherwise.

The dates that frame the weekend

A handful of fixed dates decide most of the plan, and several of them fall within a few days of the holiday.

  • Thursday, November 26: Thanksgiving. The Ahwahnee serves its holiday dinner from noon to 8 p.m.
  • Sunday, November 29: Curry Village closes, and stays closed through December 10. The same day is the last on which Camp 4 takes reservations; after it the campground goes first-come, first-served.
  • Monday, November 30: the last day the Mariposa Grove shuttle can run. The park says it closes on or before that date, depending on conditions.
  • Not a fee-free day: the park's 2026 fee-free days for US residents include Veterans Day, November 11, but not Thanksgiving. The standard vehicle pass is $35, and visitors who are not US residents pay an additional $100 per person aged 16 and older unless they hold an annual or America the Beautiful pass.
  • No entrance reservation: the park announced it would not use a timed reservation system in 2026, so the gate is not the constraint. The roads and the rooms are.

The weather and the light

The Park Service's 1991 to 2020 normals for Yosemite Valley, at about 4,000 feet, give November an average high of 56°F and an average low of 33°F, with 4.1 inches of precipitation. That is a big step from October, which averages 71°F and 1.6 inches, and the park's own summary of the climate explains why: Yosemite receives 95 percent of its precipitation between October and May, and more than 75 percent of it between November and March. Late November is the front edge of the storm season. A Thanksgiving weekend can be four days of dry, cold sun or a storm that leaves snow on the Valley floor, and nobody can say which more than a week or so out.

The days are short. The US Naval Observatory's calculation for Yosemite Valley gives sunrise at 6:50 a.m. and sunset at 4:41 p.m. Pacific time on Thanksgiving Day. Those figures assume a level horizon, and the Valley does not have one: the walls take direct sun off much of the floor well before the official sunset. Count on about nine usable hours of daylight, and plan the long walk for the middle of the day.

One compensation: the full moon falls on Tuesday, November 24, and on Thanksgiving night the moon is still 93 percent lit, rising at 6:49 p.m. On a clear night that is bright enough to wash out most stars. Come another week for stargazing.

What will be open: the road record

Yosemite Valley and Wawona are reachable by car all year, the park says, and they are the core of a Thanksgiving trip. The rest of the map is decided by the high roads, and the park's dataset of historical opening and closing dates is unusually clear about Thanksgiving week.

Tioga Road will almost certainly be closed. In every season from 2016 through 2025 it had closed for the winter before Thanksgiving. The latest of those closings was November 20, in 2018; the earliest was October 21, in 2021. The park's general guidance is that the road closes "usually by sometime in November," and once it does, no vehicles are allowed between Crane Flat and Tioga Pass. Tenaya Lake, Olmsted Point and Tuolumne Meadows are out, and so is any route that crosses the Sierra through the park.

Glacier Point Road will very probably be closed, too. In the nine seasons from 2016 through 2025 in which the road opened at all (it stayed shut in 2022 for a rehabilitation project), it had closed for the winter before Thanksgiving in all but one. The exception was 2021, when it held until December 7. And on Thanksgiving there is no winter version of the road yet: the park plows it as far as the Badger Pass Ski Area from mid-December through early April, so the plowing, the ski area and its shuttle all start weeks after the holiday.

In 2026 the road had a second reason to be closed. As of the park's October 4 update, the Dome Fire, burning northeast of Wawona, covered 6,736 acres and was 40 percent contained, and Glacier Point Road was closed for smoke and firefighting operations. That is seven weeks before the holiday and says little about late November, but if a fire is still active when you go, what a wildfire closes, and what stays open covers the bookings question.

Glacier Point is still reachable on foot. The Four Mile Trail climbs from the Valley floor to the point, 4.8 miles one way with 3,200 feet of gain by the park's figures, and its record around the holiday is good: it was open on Thanksgiving Day in nine of the ten seasons from 2016 through 2025, the exception being 2022, when it closed on November 11. With nine hours of daylight it is a full, early-start day, and not one to begin after lunch.

The Mariposa Grove is the late-season bonus. The free shuttle from the Welcome Plaza near the South Entrance runs through November in 2026, weather permitting, from 8 a.m. to 3:30 p.m., with the last bus leaving the grove at 5 p.m. That makes Thanksgiving weekend one of the last chances of the year to ride in. From December 1 there is no shuttle, and the way in is a two-mile walk with about 500 feet of gain, on the Washburn Trail or the grove road. The Mariposa Grove guide has the trail and timing detail. The grove sits near Wawona, so check the fire status in the park's alerts before driving down.

Hetch Hetchy is the other day trip. The park lists its road as open from sunrise to sunset and closed during snow, which in practice means the drive depends on the week's weather.

Chains: the rule that catches holiday drivers

A Thanksgiving trip is the first trip of the year for many visitors on which tire chains become a real question. The park's rule is short: within a designated chain control area, every vehicle must have chains or cables in it, even if the vehicle is exempt from putting them on. Under R-1, a car, pickup or SUV under 6,000 pounds with snow tires on at least two drive wheels may go without mounting them. Under R-2, the exemption narrows to four-wheel or all-wheel drive vehicles under 6,500 pounds with snow tires on all four wheels and the drive engaged. Under R-3, every vehicle chains up. The park adds that many battery-electric trucks and some large electric SUVs exceed those weight limits and must chain up regardless.

The penalties are real. The park lists a citation of up to $5,000 for not putting chains on when required, and warns that a driver caught without chains may have to call a tow truck to supply them, at a cost that can run to a few hundred dollars and a wait that can run to several hours. Rental cars are not exempt, and the park notes that rental companies generally do not provide chains and that chains are hard to rent: plan to buy a set that fits your tires before the drive, not at the checkpoint. The winter guide has the full explainer and which approach carries the least weather. The park's own alternative is to leave the car outside the park on Highway 140 and ride YARTS in, with one exception for this weekend below.

The dinner

The concessioner's 2026 holiday page names two Thanksgiving meals, and publishes the details for one.

The Ahwahnee Dining Room serves Thanksgiving dinner on November 26 from noon to 8 p.m., at $165 for an adult and $82.50 for a child, tax and gratuity included. The published menu centers on carved prime rib, roasted turkey and carved ham, with sides and desserts, and wine can be pre-ordered. Reservations are by phone at 888-413-8869. The room's ordinary dinner dress code asks for long pants and a collared shirt, or a dress, skirt, or long pants with a blouse, and no hiking clothes, shorts, T-shirts, tank tops, flip-flops or baseball caps. The holiday page does not address attire, so ask when you book, and pack one outfit that would pass.

Base Camp Eatery, the food court at Yosemite Valley Lodge, is the second: the concessioner's page describes "a family-friendly meal" there, but when we checked in early October it listed no time, price or menu for it. Treat it as a likely walk-in option and confirm closer to the date.

Older guides to Thanksgiving in Yosemite also list a dinner at the Wawona Hotel. That is out of date: the hotel has been closed since December 2, 2024, and has no reopening date. If you want a kitchen of your own instead, the Valley's cook-your-own option, Housekeeping Camp, closed for the season on October 12. That leaves a campsite, or a rented cabin or house with a kitchen, which in practice means Wawona or a gateway town. The rest of the park's food, from the Ahwahnee Bar to the grocery stores, is in where to eat in Yosemite.

Where to sleep

Inside the park the choice is narrower than at any other time of year that still has rooms. Three concessioner properties operate in the Valley that weekend: the Ahwahnee, Yosemite Valley Lodge and Curry Village, which runs a reduced operation in winter. White Wolf Lodge, Tuolumne Meadows Lodge, Housekeeping Camp and the Wawona Hotel are all closed. In Wawona, The Redwoods in Yosemite rents private cabins and homes inside the boundary, booked through its own site rather than the concessioner.

Curry Village's closing date matters for this weekend in particular. The concessioner's Curry Village page says it will not be open from November 29 through December 10, 2026, so a Curry stay over Thanksgiving ends on the 29th at the latest. Confirm your departure date when you book. The concessioner's winter offers, which put the Ahwahnee from $389 on select two-night stays and Curry Village from $95 a night, are for select dates, and the offer pages do not say whether Thanksgiving is among them. Price the weekend at the ordinary rate and treat any discount as luck.

One policy is worth knowing for a sold-out weekend. The concessioner's lodging pages ask for a deposit of the first night and refund it only on cancellations received at least seven days before arrival. Anyone holding a room they may not use has a reason to release it before that deadline, so the days just before November 19 are a sensible time to search again for Thanksgiving arrival. That is a reading of the policy, not a promise of rooms.

Outside the park, the gateway towns carry the overflow: El Portal and Mariposa on Highway 140, Oakhurst and Fish Camp on Highway 41, and Groveland on Highway 120. Highway 140 is also the corridor the park names for leaving a car outside the chain controls and riding the bus in. Camping over the holiday

Camping is possible, and it is the most weather-exposed way to spend the weekend. The park's campground page says that only Upper Pines is on reservations all year. Its sites are released five months ahead on the 15th of each month at 7 a.m. Pacific, and the window for arrivals from November 15 through December 14 opened on July 15. The park notes that reservations sell out within minutes, so for 2026 the realistic route into Upper Pines now is a cancellation on Recreation.gov.

Camp 4 takes reservations one week ahead, on a rolling window, through November 29, at $10 per person per night, then goes first-come, first-served for the winter. Wawona and Hodgdon Meadow are open all year and run first-come, first-served in winter, and the park warns that the first-come campgrounds can fill during holidays and weekends. Arrive early on Wednesday, not Thursday afternoon. Sleeping in a vehicle is not allowed anywhere in the park outside a campsite you have registered for.

Pack for the normals, not the forecast: an average low of 33°F means nights below freezing are routine.

Getting there without a car

YARTS runs its Highway 140 route year-round between Merced and the Valley, and its winter schedule runs from October 1, 2026, to May 28, 2027. The first run from Merced arrives at Yosemite Valley Lodge at 7:13 a.m. But YARTS lists Thanksgiving Day among the holidays on which it does not operate, and its Highway 140 page prints the date as "November 27, 2026," which is the Friday. Until the agency clears that up, do not plan to ride on either day without calling first. Inside the Valley, the free shuttle operates year-round.

Four short days, planned

Arrive Wednesday. On a holiday weekend a first-come campsite goes to whoever got there first, and a day in hand absorbs a storm or a chain control on the drive. If a storm is forecast, drive in ahead of it.

Give Thursday to the Valley. A morning walk on the floor, the meadows and the river while the light is low, then dinner. If you booked the Ahwahnee, the noon-to-8 window lets you take a late lunch slot and still have the afternoon light outside, or an early evening one after a full day out.

Spend Friday or Saturday on the big outing. The Four Mile Trail to Glacier Point if it is open and the weather is dry, with an early start against the 4:41 sunset. The Mariposa Grove by shuttle, the last weekend of the year it runs, if the fire status near Wawona allows. Hetch Hetchy if the road is open.

Leave Sunday, with chains in the car. If you are in Curry Village, Sunday is checkout regardless. Read the conditions page the morning you drive in and the morning you leave: roads, chain controls, trail closures and the fire alerts all change by the day in late November, and it collects the park's live feeds and phone numbers in one place.

The short version: book the dinner and the room now, expect the high roads to be closed, carry chains, and treat everything open beyond the Valley as a bonus the weather may or may not grant.

July and August are when Yosemite is most open and most full. Every road is usually clear of snow, the high country is in season, and the Park Service counts about 15 percent of the year's visits in each month. The Valley floor averages 89°F in the afternoon; Tuolumne Meadows, up Tioga Road at 8,600 feet, averages about 70.

The trade is simple to state. The waterfalls are past their peak, the Valley is hot and crowded at midday, and in August the air is often smoky. The high country, the rivers and the early morning are at their best.

This is the evergreen guide to both months. The numbers below come from the Park Service and the park's concessioner; where a figure is for 2026 only, it says so. Confirm anything dated before a 2027 trip.

The short version

  • Be through the gate before 8 am. Valley parking is usually full after that.
  • Spend the hot afternoon high, in Tuolumne Meadows, or in the river.
  • Be off summits and ridges by early afternoon. Storms build after noon.
  • Check the air quality, especially in August.

WEATHER

Two climates, one road apart

The Park Service's 1991 to 2020 averages put Yosemite Valley, at 4,000 feet, at a high of 89°F and a low of 57°F in both July and August, with almost no rain: 0.3 inches in July and 0.2 in August. Tuolumne Meadows, at 8,600 feet, averages 72°F in July and 70°F in August, and drops to the high thirties at night. The park gets 95 percent of its precipitation between October and May.

An average is not a ceiling. On the Valley floor the granite holds the heat through the afternoon, and the climbs out of it, the Mist Trail and the Yosemite Falls Trail, are where summer heat puts hikers in the clinic. Our heat safety guide covers which trails run hottest and when to be on them. The Park Service's own minimum is a quart of water every two hours.

Thunderstorms. The Park Service says storms are most common from June through September and most common in the afternoon, and that from July to September the North American monsoon can carry moisture into the Sierra Nevada. Its advice for the backcountry: be above treeline or on the rim in the morning and summit peaks before noon. If you hear thunder, a storm is within 10 miles. There is no safe shelter outside; the only safe places are a substantial building or a hard-topped vehicle. In July 1985 lightning on Half Dome killed two people and injured three.

CROWDS

The two busiest months of the year

By the Park Service's monthly averages for 2010 to 2024, July is the busiest month in Yosemite at 624,559 visits, and August is next at 607,000. Each is about 15 percent of the year. Nearly three quarters of the year's visitors come between May and October.

The crowd is not spread evenly. The Park Service's traffic page says Saturdays and holiday weekends are the worst, that Valley parking is usually full after 8 am, and that once the lots fill, traffic can back up for miles. Its advice is to arrive early, stay late, and come midweek. Our crowds by month guide has the full table and the five places the crowd piles up.

No entrance reservation in 2026. The Park Service said it would not use a timed entrance reservation system in 2026. It has not said what it will do in summer 2027; check its reservations page before you plan around either answer. The entrance fee is $35 per private vehicle, good for seven consecutive days. The park also lists a $100 per-person surcharge for non-US residents 16 and older who do not hold an annual or America the Beautiful pass. The 2026 fee-free days for US citizens and residents included July 3 to 5 and August 25; the 2027 list was not posted when we checked.

WATERFALLS AND RIVERS

Less in the falls, more in the river

The waterfalls. The Park Service says peak runoff usually comes in May or June. It lists Yosemite Falls as flowing roughly November through July, and it says some falls are often only a trickle or completely dry by August. Vernal Fall, below the Mist Trail, narrows in mid to late summer and can split into two or three strands. If the falls are the reason for the trip, July is the last month that gives you a fair chance at Yosemite Falls, and June is the better bet.

The river. The same drop in snowmelt is what makes the Merced swimmable. Our guide to swimming in the Merced puts the usual window at about mid-July into September, and says what matters is the flow on the day you are standing there. The Park Service's warnings hold all summer: the water is extremely cold even on a hot day, even a slow current will take you where you may not want to go, and submerged branches and rock gaps can hold a swimmer under. Swimming is closed at Emerald Pool above Vernal Fall. Obey the signs that say no swimming.

THE HIGH COUNTRY

Go up when the Valley is at its hottest

July and August are the high country's months. The Park Service says Tioga Road is typically open to vehicles from late May or June until sometime in November, and Glacier Point Road from sometime in May into November. In 2026 Tioga Road opened on May 15 and Glacier Point Road on May 9. Both dates move with the snowpack.

Tuolumne Meadows sits at 8,600 feet, and its average highs run 17 to 19 degrees under the Valley floor's, which is the best argument for driving up on a hot day. Our guide to Tuolumne Meadows in a day has the walks, the lakes and the order to do them in. The Park Service's practical notes: there is no gas in Tuolumne Meadows, and along Tioga Road it says to bring your own food and water. The Tuolumne Meadows shuttle runs typically mid-June to early September, between Tioga Pass and Olmsted Point by way of Tenaya Lake. YARTS runs its Highway 120 East bus daily in July through September, conditions permitting, between Yosemite Village and Mammoth Lakes with stops at Crane Flat, White Wolf and Tuolumne Meadows.

Where to sleep up high. Tuolumne Meadows Lodge, 69 canvas tent cabins without electricity, listed a 2026 season of June 5 to September 13, conditions permitting. White Wolf Lodge is listed as closed. Tuolumne Meadows Campground is open roughly July to late September; the Park Service releases half its sites two months ahead and half two weeks ahead.

Glacier Point. Glacier Point Road is open through both months in a normal year. Yosemite Hospitality's Glacier Point Tour leaves the Yosemite Valley Lodge lobby at 8:30 am and 1:30 pm; its 2026 prices were $72.50 round trip and $28.50 one way for adults 13 and over. The Glacier Point guide covers parking and when to go.

SMOKE AND FIRE

August is the start of smoke season

The Park Service says smoky conditions are most common in August and September, and that bad air can last one to three weeks in a typical year. Wildfire smoke causes the park's worst days for visibility and air quality, and ozone, which most often spikes on hot, stagnant summer days, adds to it. The park posts current readings from its monitors in Yosemite Village and at Turtleback Dome. Our guide to Yosemite during smoke season explains where smoke settles and how to plan around it; the short version is that the Valley traps it and the high country is often clearer.

Campfires. From May through September, wood fires in Yosemite Valley and at Hodgdon Meadow are allowed only between 5 pm and 10 pm, and must be completely out by 10. Quiet hours are 10 pm to 6 am. In summer 2026 the park also put fire restrictions in effect below 8,000 feet: no wood or charcoal fires, twig stoves included, outside open designated campgrounds, open picnic areas that allow them and a few other exceptions, and smoking only in an enclosed vehicle, a designated smoking area, those same campgrounds and picnic areas, or while stopped on a bare spot at least three feet across. Restrictions are set each season; check the park's fire restrictions page.

The Fourth of July. The Park Service has said plainly in its holiday releases that no fireworks are allowed in Yosemite, because they disturb wildlife and raise the risk of wildfire.

BOOKING

What to book, and how far ahead

Lodging in the park opens 366 days ahead, the Park Service says, and for a July or August stay that is when it goes. If you cannot find a room inside, the park's own advice is the gateway towns.

Campgrounds. Upper, Lower and North Pines in the Valley, Wawona and Hodgdon Meadow release sites on Recreation.gov five months ahead, on the 15th of each month, at 7 am Pacific, and the Park Service says they sell out within minutes. Tuolumne Meadows releases half its sites two months ahead and half two weeks ahead. Bridalveil Creek, White Wolf and Yosemite Creek open sometime in July and close in early September; Crane Flat, Tamarack Flat and Porcupine Flat run from July to early October, all on a rolling two-week window.

Half Dome. The cables are typically up from the Friday before Memorial Day to the day after the second Monday in October, so they are up through both months. A permit is required while they are. The preseason lottery runs through March; a smaller daily lottery takes applications two days ahead. Each application costs $10, nonrefundable, and each hiker on a winning permit pays a $10 recreation fee. The details are on our Half Dome lottery page.

When the park is full

In-park rooms book through Yosemite Hospitality, and the Park Service says reservations open 366 days ahead. When they are gone, a search shows what is left in the gateway towns, within driving distance of the gate you plan to use before 8 am.

JULY OR AUGUST

If you can choose

July

  • The busiest month of the year, on the Park Service's average.
  • Yosemite Falls is in its last month on the Park Service's flow calendar.
  • The Fourth of July holiday weekend. Fireworks are not allowed in the park.
  • Afternoon thunderstorms in the high country.

August

  • About 17,500 fewer visits than July, on the same average.
  • Some falls are only a trickle or dry.
  • The start of the park's smokiest two months.
  • The same Valley averages: 89°F high, 57°F low.

Pick July for the last of Yosemite Falls and a slightly lower chance of smoke. Expect the year's biggest crowd, and the Fourth of July weekend at the top of it.

Pick August for a warm Merced and the high country at full summer. Expect the falls to be thin and the air to be a gamble, and keep a high-country day in hand for a smoky Valley.

Either way, the day has the same shape: in through the gate before 8 am, the hard hike or the Valley sights in the morning, the afternoon high or in the water, and the Valley again in the evening when the light comes off the walls. The month after this is September, when the crowd eases after Labor Day.

How hot is Yosemite in July and August?

The Park Service's 1991 to 2020 averages put Yosemite Valley, at 4,000 feet, at a high of 89°F and a low of 57°F in both months. Tuolumne Meadows, at 8,600 feet, averages a high of 72°F in July and 70°F in August, with lows near 39°F and 37°F.

Is July or August busier in Yosemite?

July, by a little. The Park Service's monthly averages for 2010 to 2024 are 624,559 visits in July and 607,000 in August, each about 15 percent of the year. Saturdays and holiday weekends are the busiest days, and Valley parking is usually full after 8 am.

Do you need a reservation to enter Yosemite in summer?

Not in 2026: the Park Service said it would not use a timed entrance reservation system that year. It has not announced its plan for summer 2027, so check the park's reservations page before you go. The entrance fee is $35 per vehicle, valid for seven days.

Are the waterfalls running in July and August?

Thinly. The Park Service says peak runoff is usually in May or June, that Yosemite Falls flows roughly November through July, and that some falls are often only a trickle or completely dry by August.

Is Tioga Road open in July and August?

In a normal year, yes. The Park Service says Tioga Road is typically open to vehicles from late May or June until sometime in November. There is no gas in Tuolumne Meadows.

Is there smoke in Yosemite in August?

Often. The Park Service says smoky conditions are most common in August and September, and that bad air can last one to three weeks in a typical year. Check the park's air quality monitors before you go.

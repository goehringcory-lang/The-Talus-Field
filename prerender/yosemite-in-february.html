February in Yosemite is two trips with a fence between them. For most of the month the park keeps January's habits: short days, wet storms, the winter hotel rates in force, and a Valley floor with room on it. Then, for about a week and a half in the middle, the firefall and Presidents' Day weekend arrive together, the hotels stop discounting, and the road along El Capitan fills with people waiting for sunset. Which half you book decides the crowd, the price, and whether the evenings are yours. Start there.

What follows is the month as the park's own records describe it: the weather normals, what is open, the ski area's recent seasons, the campground calendar, and the dates the concessioner publishes. How Horsetail Fall works, and how good the odds are on any given evening, is in the Horsetail Fall guide, and this piece does not repeat it. It is about the rest of the trip.

The weather, in numbers

The Park Service's 1991 to 2020 normals for Yosemite Valley, at about 4,000 feet, give February an average high of 51°F and an average low of 30°F, with 6.5 inches of precipitation. That makes February the second wettest month of the year in the Valley, behind January's 7.0 inches, and three degrees warmer by day. The nights still sit at freezing on average. The park says it receives more than 75 percent of its precipitation between November and March, so a February trip is still a trip into the storm season.

Whether a Valley storm arrives as rain or snow is decided storm by storm; the park's phrase is that the Valley "can be rainy or snowy in any given winter storm." Up high there is no question. Tuolumne Meadows, at 8,600 feet, averages a February high of 39°F and a low of 12°F, colder by day than January, and the park describes most of Yosemite as blanketed in snow from about November through May. Pack for a wet day and a cold clear one, and plan on frost every night.

What is open in February

Yosemite Valley and Wawona are reachable by car all year, and they are the whole of a February trip by road. Everything above them was decided in November.

Tioga Road is closed, and Tuolumne Meadows, Tenaya Lake, and Olmsted Point with it. Glacier Point Road is plowed only to Badger Pass: from mid-December through early April the park clears the road as far as the Badger Pass Ski Area and no farther, so the drive-up view from Glacier Point is not part of any February trip. The point is still reachable on skis, which is a long day, and the road beyond Badger becomes a groomed track for exactly that purpose.

The Mariposa Grove is open on foot. The grove road is closed in winter, and the park says it usually opens by sometime in April. Until then the Washburn Trail is a two-mile route from the Welcome Plaza to the grove with 500 feet of elevation gain, which the park warns may be snowy or icy enough that skis or snowshoes are the right tools. A clear February day among snow-loaded sequoias is one of the best things the month has, and the walk-in plan is in the Mariposa Grove guide.

The trails close the way they did in January. The Mist Trail beside Vernal Fall, the stretch of the John Muir Trail between Clark Point and the top of Nevada Fall, and the Four Mile Trail above its winter gate below Union Point are the park's routine winter closures, and February is the middle of their season. The Yosemite Falls Trail to Columbia Rock, which takes direct winter sun on its lower switchbacks, is the month's most reliable climb. The full list of what stays open, from the Valley Loop to Hetch Hetchy, is in the winter hiking guide.

The fence in the middle: firefall weeks and Presidents' Day

The Park Service gives the firefall's window as mid to late February, on clear evenings when Horsetail Fall is flowing, and says the effect is rare and that haze or cloud can erase it. The same weeks hold the month's one federal holiday. Presidents' Day falls on Monday, February 15, 2027, which puts the long weekend squarely inside the firefall window.

The concessioner marks the overlap in its rate calendar. Its Yosemite Valley Lodge winter offer for 2026 to 2027 runs from November 29 through March 13 and carries two blackout windows: December 23 through January 2, and February 12 through 20, 2027. The site's winter guide records the same February blackout on the Ahwahnee and Curry Village offers. That is the hotel company saying, in its own language, which nine nights of February it expects to sell at full price. The rest of the month is discount season.

The park sets its firefall rules each season, and as of this writing it has published none for 2027. For February 2026 it required no reservation for the park or the Horsetail Fall area. It prohibited parking, stopping, and unloading passengers on Northside Drive between Lower Yosemite Fall and the El Capitan Crossover, closed one lane of that road to cars so pedestrians could walk it, and warned that on busy weekends Northside Drive might close completely for about half an hour after sunset. Viewers parked at Yosemite Falls, or at Yosemite Village or Curry Village with the free shuttle, and walked 1.5 miles each way to the viewing area near the El Capitan Picnic Area. Whatever the park announces for 2027 will be on the firefall page; plan on rules of that shape, and on a cold walk back in the dark.

Two more February details belong to the calendar. In 2026 the park system's first free entrance day of the year was Presidents' Day, February 16, and from 2026 those days apply to US citizens and residents only. The 2027 list has not been published. And the park dropped its timed-entry reservation system for 2026; whether it returns for 2027 has not been announced, so check the park's reservations page for the year you travel. The standard entrance pass is $35 for a private vehicle, valid for seven consecutive days. Visitors who are not US residents should read the international visitors page first, because the park adds a $100 per-person fee for nonresidents 16 and older.

Badger Pass in February

On paper, February is the heart of the ski season. The concessioner describes the Badger Pass downhill area as operating daily from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m., mid-December to mid-March, conditions permitting, and gives the tubing hill's anticipated dates as January through March, because the hill is built entirely from natural snow. The free park shuttle between the Valley and Badger Pass runs whenever the ski area's facilities are open, which the park puts at typically mid-December through March.

The last two winters show what "conditions permitting" means. In February 2025 the concessioner and the Park Service announced that Badger Pass would not open at all for the 2024 to 2025 season, because the snow had come too late to make a short season worthwhile. In 2025 to 2026 the Nordic center opened on January 9 and the lifts on January 16, and the area closed for the season on March 18, after a week of warm days and nights without a freeze melted the snowpack. In both years February was the month a ski trip depended on, and in one of them there was nothing to ski. The 2026 to 2027 opening date has not been announced.

So February is the best month in the calendar for Badger, and still not a promise. Before building a day around the ski area, the tubing hill, or the ranger-led snowshoe walks that start there, call the concessioner's Snow Phone at 209/372-1000 or check its Badger Pass page. Down in the Valley, the Curry Village ice rink needs no snow at all; the park gives its season as mid-November to mid-March, conditions permitting, and the concessioner lists the 2026 to 2027 opening date as still to be determined.

Driving in: chains, still

The park's guidance is to expect chain requirements from November through March, and February is in the middle of that window. The rule that catches visitors is about carrying, not mounting: whenever a chain control is in effect, every vehicle must have chains or cables in it, four-wheel drives and rentals included. Under R1, a car or pickup under 6,000 pounds with snow tires on at least two drive wheels may skip mounting them; under R2, a vehicle under 6,500 pounds with four-wheel or all-wheel drive engaged and snow tires on all four wheels may skip them; under R3 there are no exceptions. Failing to put chains on when required carries a citation of up to $5,000.

Route choice matters. The park says the Wawona Road (Highway 41), the Big Oak Flat Road (Highway 120 from the west), and Badger Pass Road need chains more often than the El Portal Road (Highway 140), the Valley's own roads, and the Hetch Hetchy Road. Highway 140 is also the one route YARTS runs all year, from Merced through Mariposa, Midpines, and El Portal. The road-conditions recording, 209/372-0200, then 1, then 1, is the call to make the morning you drive.

Where to sleep in February

In the park, the Ahwahnee, Yosemite Valley Lodge, and Curry Village carry the month, as they did in January. Book the firefall nights first if you want them: February 12 through 20 is the stretch the concessioner expects to fill, and Presidents' Day weekend falls inside it. Outside that window, the winter rates return and a midweek room is not hard to find.

Camping in February is a short list. Upper Pines is the only campground on reservations all year, and it sells on the park's monthly calendar at 7 a.m. Pacific, five months out, so the February 2027 dates are already on sale: arrivals from January 15 through February 14 went on sale September 15, 2026, and arrivals from February 15 through March 14 on October 15. Presidents' Day weekend straddles the two, with the Saturday and Sunday in the first and the Monday in the second. Camp 4, Wawona, and Hodgdon Meadow are first come, first served in winter, and the park warns that they can fill on holidays and weekends, which describes the middle of February exactly. Plan for nights below freezing and a tent that can carry snow.

Outside the park, the towns on Highway 140 put you on the lowest road and the year-round bus line, and that is the strongest argument for Mariposa or El Portal in February. The town-by-town comparison is on the lodging board.

A naturalist's February

The waterfalls are coming back. The park's account of winter is that water levels tend to be low, but that once rain and snow have fallen, Yosemite Falls begins flowing again, though not at springtime levels, and after the two wettest months of the year that usually means a fall with some voice to it. The firefall depends on the same water. Horsetail Fall is a seasonal stream off the top of El Capitan, and a dry winter can leave it too thin to light.

The high country is at its deepest, and the park's naturalists have long gone up to look at it. The March 1932 Yosemite Nature Notes reported that the mountaineer Norman Clyde spent the first week of that February at Glacier Point, snowed in by a heavy storm. On one of his snowshoe trips he flushed a grouse from a snowbank and found that the bird had been sheltering in a hole about two feet deep in the fresh snow, a habit the bulletin called regularly observed in the East and rare in the Sierra. The same issue noted that bears were out and about through that heavy-snow winter, as in other years. The park's food-storage rules are written without a season, so a February visitor stores food exactly as a July one does.

How to build a February trip

Pick your half of the month first. If the firefall is the point, come between February 12 and 20, book the room before anything else, and accept that the evenings will be crowded and the walk out long. If the point is the park, come in the first ten days of the month or the last week, midweek, when the winter rates are in force and the Valley is as quiet as it gets.

Then check the snow, not the calendar. February is the likeliest month for Badger Pass to be running, and the last two winters show it is still not certain. Confirm before you drive up. If the ski area is closed, the Valley floor, Columbia Rock, and the walk into the Mariposa Grove make a full trip without it, and by the end of the month the season is already turning toward March, when the waterfalls start to build.

Read the conditions the morning of. Chain controls, road closures, trail gates, and the ski area change by the day. The conditions page collects the park's live feeds and phone numbers in one place. Come up 140 with chains in the trunk, keep one day free for the storm, and spend the clear day after it outside.

February rooms depend on which half

From February 12 through 20 the concessioner blacks out its winter rates for the firefall and Presidents' Day weekend, and the in-park rooms go first. Either side of that window, the winter discounts are back and the gateway towns on Highway 140 are quiet. A search on your dates shows which February you are dealing with.
